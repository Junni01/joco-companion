# Historical humour and absurdity — East India Company / India inspiration catalogue

**Research inventory · 26 October 2026**  
**Status:** historical inspiration and proposed writing opportunities only. **No production dialogue approved**, no narrative trigger implemented or authorized, no change to M2B-1 or gameplay. The examples concern *historical people in the real world*, not characters or verified events of the game's alternate-history campaign.

## How to use this catalogue

For each episode, distinguish (a) **documented historical kernel**, (b) **why the situation lends itself to satire**, (c) **possible fictional narrative trigger**, and (d) **limitations and traps**. Character voices should be original, culturally and chronologically plausible, and specific to the *recorded campaign's own events*. Do not import a historical scandal, payment, duel, death, fictional royal meeting or looted artifact as something that happened in a given game unless its story consequence is separately introduced and committed at a compatible actual event. Historical inspiration is *not* historical event generation.

Confidence legend: **A — institution/primary document confirms core**; **B — credible historical study or contemporary-related account, detail warrants further checking**. Confidence is about the **narrow core fact** only, never about proposed comic interpretations or fictional lines. Record sources as stable URLs + institutional archive identifiers where available.

### HHI-01 — Robert Clive is astonished by his own moderation
- **Period / people:** Robert Clive, parliamentary scrutiny, May 1773.
- **Documented core — A/B:** Parliament records that Clive vigorously defended his India conduct over three days in the House of Commons. The celebrated quotation, commonly given as *“I stand astonished at my own moderation”*, appears in historical accounts; consult a parliamentary speech edition before treating any exact extended wording as verbatim.
- **Comic mechanism:** The wealthy accused official presents all the wealth he *could* have taken as evidence of exemplary restraint. Vast opportunity for impropriety becomes his principal defence.
- **Fictional opportunity:** Revenue shareholders, parliamentary allegations, actual Blackmail scandal or exceptionally self-satisfied fictional Company adviser. A rival can politely calculate the much larger amount allegedly not stolen. Distinguish recorded scandal from an actual proven crime.
- **Avoid:** an automatic assumption that any Company conquest yielded private loot, or that a fictional player officer received a secret payment. Do not put Clive's literal line in a new fictional person's mouth as a documented quote.
- **Sources:** [UK Parliament — East India Company and public opinion](https://www.parliament.uk/about/living-heritage/evolutionofparliament/legislativescrutiny/parliament-and-empire/parliament-and-the-american-colonies-before-1765/the-east-india-company-and-public-opinion/); [historical biography preserved at IGNCA (secondary account of the wording)](https://ignca.gov.in/Asi_data/1808.pdf).
- **Related backlog:** F03, F05, D11, E08.

### HHI-02 — A Governor-General and a senior councillor settle their dispute with pistols
- **Date / people:** 17 August 1780, Warren Hastings and Philip Francis.
- **Documented core — A:** British Library catalogue for **Add MS 39878** identifies Hastings's own diary account under 15/17 August 1780. **Add MS 41606** includes his letter to his wife on the eve of the duel, dated 16 August 1780.
- **Comic mechanism:** A bitter constitutional/administrative quarrel between senior officials escalates into a duel rather than an orderly memorandum.
- **Fictional opportunity:** Very rare Company adviser rivalry, high-level scandal or an escalation following multiple evidenced disagreements. Secretary proudly announces a 'settlement', then reluctantly clarifies the arms used.
- **Avoid:** portraying all policy disputes as duels or introducing an unreported officer casualty. Dramatic injury, dismissal or death requires its own approved narrative-only state transition and well-scoped trigger.
- **Sources:** [British Library Add MS 39878](https://searcharchives.bl.uk/catalog/040-002059733), [British Library Add MS 41606](https://searcharchives.bl.uk/catalog/040-002085291).
- **Related backlog:** E01, E08, E09, A07.

### HHI-03 — Tipu's Tiger, the mechanical satire of a European soldier being mauled
- **Period / people:** late eighteenth century, Mysore court of Tipu Sultan; seized in 1799.
- **Documented core — A:** The V&A preserves the wooden tiger automaton: moving human arm, internal organ and sounds approximating a victim's groans. It was made for Tipu Sultan and, after the 1799 assault on Seringapatam, transported to London and exhibited in the Company's Indian Museum.
- **Comic mechanism:** A formidable ruler commissions a political insult with literal moving parts, while later Company officials attempt to inventory and display it as a prestigious object.
- **Fictional opportunity:** The researched **Mysore treasure candidate pool**, court eccentricity, Company prize clerk, a London museum or shareholder's discomfort about exhibiting an anti-English work.
- **Avoid:** automatically awarding the actual historical object in scenarios where it was never made; equating a fictionally conquered Mysore region with the real Seringapatam campaign; using the victim's violence as slapstick.
- **Sources:** [V&A — Tipu's Tiger](https://www.vam.ac.uk/articles/tipus-tiger), [V&A — a bust of Marie Antoinette from Tipu's court](https://www.vam.ac.uk/articles/the-tiger-and-the-queen-a-bust-of-marie-antoinette-from-the-court-of-tipu-sultan).
- **Related backlog:** H03, C02, D06.

### HHI-04 — The Company petitions for exact compensation after the Boston Tea Party
- **Date / people:** 16 December 1773 tea destruction; East India Company petition dated 16 February 1774.
- **Documented core — A:** The UK National Archives preserves the Directors' compensation memorial, catalogue **CO 5/247**. It tabulates **340 tea chests** and the claimed value **£9,659 6s 4d**. The 1773 Tea Act intended to assist the financially troubled Company but increased colonial opposition.
- **Comic mechanism:** A transatlantic crisis is translated by the corporation into a meticulously costed reimbursement request.
- **Fictional opportunity:** London shareholders, commercial clerk, Parliament and the self-interested MP react to an actual recorded commercial crisis, requested indemnity or disastrous legislation. Can inspire bureaucracy but is **outside the India board map**.
- **Avoid:** claiming the Boston Tea Party happened in the campaign, or that the Company's actual in-game tea, shares or cash balances are known. Treat the historical episode as background inspiration, not a playable US-region event.
- **Sources:** [UK National Archives — compensation request](https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/explore-the-collection/stories/request-for-compensation-for-the-boston-tea-party/), [UK National Archives — Boston Tea Party](https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/education/teaching-resources/boston-tea-party/).
- **Related backlog:** F03, F05, E08, C02.

### HHI-05 — The Batta Mutiny: officers object collectively to reduced allowances
- **Date / place:** May 1766, Bengal Army; Robert Clive.
- **Documented core — A/B:** Transcriptions/catalogue pointers to **IOR/P/251/55** and 1766 Madras Military Proceedings record the collective Bengal officer resignation attempt over withdrawn 'double batta' and the transfer of officers from Madras to deal with it.
- **Comic mechanism:** An army can defeat formidable opponents but nearly falls apart over a disagreement about how much it should pay its own officers.
- **Fictional opportunity:** Army secretary, Chairman's clerk, cost-minded shareholder or internal adviser dissent on genuine military setbacks. Can be applied to fictional officer dissatisfaction after suitably sourced campaign history.
- **Avoid:** asserting a battle mutiny, printed-game regimental strike, military disobedience or paid bonuses merely because an Army is exhausted. No rule effect or arbitrary officeholder dismissal.
- **Sources:** [FIBIS record on Madras officers; original India Office reference IOR/P/251/55](https://search.fibis.org/bin/aps_detail.php?id=2658878), [FIBIS contemporary Bengal officer roster](https://search.fibis.org/bin/aps_detail.php?id=2658868).
- **Related backlog:** E02, E08, E09, F03.

### HHI-06 — A Company surgeon becomes too useful to the Mughal emperor
- **Date / people:** Mughal court, 1715–1717; surgeon William Hamilton and emperor Farrukhsiyar.
- **Documented core — B:** A historical medical paper quoting letters from the 1717 embassy describes an imperial attempt to retain Hamilton as surgeon at the farewell audience on **23 May 1717**, causing significant anxiety to the Company delegation. The medical record should be checked for its exact historical documentary basis before adapting specific correspondence.
- **Comic mechanism:** The embassy's indispensable professional is suddenly more valuable to the host emperor than to his own Company; bureaucracy must reconcile professional indispensability with its personnel plans.
- **Fictional opportunity:** Special Envoy, regional court and Director's secretary; a genuine peaceful diplomatic interaction could inspire a comic standoff about a cherished adviser or doctor.
- **Avoid:** repeating the simplified claim that one cure *single-handedly* won every farman or Company trading privilege. The earlier anecdote that directors were planning to **dismiss** Hamilton needs independent direct documentary verification; **do not promote that detail to a verified fact yet**.
- **Source:** [William Hamilton and the Embassy to Delhi — medical history article (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5166139/).
- **Related backlog:** E03, D06, C02.

### HHI-07 — The elaborate prize-money bureaucracy after Seringapatam
- **Date / place:** 1799, Seringapatam, Mysore.
- **Documented core — A:** The V&A records that after the conquest a formal Prize Committee distributed proceeds from Tipu Sultan's treasury; official sales exceeded **£1.1 million** but loot outside the official process was substantial. Prize sharing was extremely unequal by rank.
- **Comic mechanism:** Confiscation and profiteering are disguised beneath exact rules, committees, auctions and solemn rank distinctions.
- **Fictional opportunity:** One-time region treasure acquisition, prize clerk, Company adviser explaining why two officers claim the same historical object; dry commentary on acquisition rather than celebrating the harm of conquest.
- **Avoid:** claiming the Company's physical game awards a share of actual Seringapatam cash or tracking specific officer dividends; treasure remains **one randomly persistent object per region and one first-Company-conquest acquisition** under our proposed app lifecycle.
- **Source:** [V&A — The Tiger and the Queen](https://www.vam.ac.uk/articles/the-tiger-and-the-queen-a-bust-of-marie-antoinette-from-the-court-of-tipu-sultan).
- **Related backlog:** H03, E08, B09.

### HHI-08 — The 'nabob' becomes a theatrical caricature
- **Date / work:** 1772, Samuel Foote's *The Nabob*.
- **Documented core — A/B:** Historical literary scholarship identifies Matthew Mite, a newly wealthy returnee from India who tries to buy cultural prestige and elite social standing; Parliament documents broader public anxiety about corrupt 'nabobs' and their influence on MPs.
- **Comic mechanism:** Someone can purchase almost anything except unquestioned respect, and regards his riches as irrefutable evidence of virtue.
- **Fictional opportunity:** The proposed rotten-borough MP, London shareholders, unconvincing historical anecdotes of returned servants and parliamentary scene arguments over private influence.
- **Avoid:** assuming every wealthy returnee was corrupt, every MP was bought, or a specific player family owns Company shares. Preserve actual published play chronology.
- **Sources:** [Cambridge University Press — *Monopolizing Knowledge*, Foote discussion](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/monopolizing-knowledge/making-of-company-science-16001813/435465C74AD62CA45E114F80A5A048D2), [UK Parliament — nabobs and public opinion](https://www.parliament.uk/about/living-heritage/evolutionofparliament/legislativescrutiny/parliament-and-empire/parliament-and-the-american-colonies-before-1765/the-east-india-company-and-public-opinion/).
- **Related backlog:** E08, F03, F05, C02.

### HHI-09 — French republican club in an Indian monarch's city
- **Dates / place:** **5 May–4 June 1797**, Seringapatam, Mysore.
- **Documented core — A:** British Library catalogue **Mss Eur D99** describes proceedings of a political club organized under François Ripaud and attended by **59 French citizens in Tipu Sultan's pay**.
- **Comic mechanism:** European republican language of fraternity and equality negotiates an awkward coexistence with a monarch's sponsorship and practical military interests.
- **Fictional opportunity:** Recurring French expedition VIPs and Indian court clients; different French commanders interpret revolutionary principles differently; an anxious court official writes a painfully diplomatic invitation.
- **Avoid:** portraying the 1797 club as proof that every actual French officer was a dedicated ideologue, or claiming French sovereignty/vassalage not present on the John Company map. Whether Tipu himself joined a club or accepted a particular revolutionary title needs distinct evidence; it is *not* established by the catalogue description alone.
- **Source:** [British Library — Mss Eur D99](https://searcharchives.bl.uk/catalog/032-002272039).
- **Related backlog:** G02, G03, G07, C02.

### HHI-10 — The corporation governs territory but can't keep its own accounts in order
- **Period:** Company financial crisis 1772, Regulating Act 1773.
- **Documented core — A:** UK Parliament records widespread criticism of badly administered Company affairs, a major **1772 financial crisis**, government intervention, parliamentary investigation and the 1773 Regulating Act.
- **Comic mechanism:** Directors representing an enormous territorial power need an emergency government rescue and submit to oversight because the corporation is incapable of managing itself.
- **Fictional opportunity:** Revenue failure and its epilogue, irrepressibly upbeat Chairman's secretary, fictional shareholders, parliamentary ridicule and recurring pro-Company MP rationalization.
- **Avoid:** claiming generic Company losses or specific in-game loans/dividends without a committed Revenue report; do not force the end-game Parliament scene if the published rules immediately end play during Revenue.
- **Sources:** [UK Parliament — Parliament and the East India Company](https://www.parliament.uk/about/living-heritage/evolutionofparliament/legislativescrutiny/parliament-and-empire/parliament-and-the-american-colonies-before-1765/parliament-and-the-east-india-company/), [UK Parliament — public opinion](https://www.parliament.uk/about/living-heritage/evolutionofparliament/legislativescrutiny/parliament-and-empire/parliament-and-the-american-colonies-before-1765/the-east-india-company-and-public-opinion/).
- **Related backlog:** F02, F03, F04, F05, E08.

This is **not a fixed writing plan**. A particularly strong historical incident may inspire several independent *fictional* templates that should not always co-occur. Prefer irony arising from long-lived characters' known history over inserting a random unrelated quip.

## Writing and runtime guardrails

1. **Never conflate a historical kernel with the game's actual timeline.** Source facts are research references, not automatically committed alternate-history events or character memories.
2. **Keep three voices distinct:** the chronicler certifies source-supported in-game outcomes; rulers are biased witnesses; fictional Company advisers are institutional interpreters and can be wrong. Humorous argument is not factual proof.
3. **Target the powerful and their paperwork.** Satirize vanity, patronage, corruption, contradictory incentives, and self-justification. Avoid framing famine, displacement, torture, conquest casualties or victims of colonial violence as punchlines.
4. **No manufactured game effects:** an amusing corruption anecdote does not establish a bribe; a war story does not prove officer death; a foreign commander is not a new foreign province.
5. **Source-aware chronology:** a 1710 scenario cannot reference the historical 1799 Tiger seizure as completed history, and a fictional veteran cannot recall a battle outside their plausible lifespan. Historical events **outside** the game's own fictional timeline are optional colour only after the Collection 10 historical-reference policy has been settled.
6. **Save/version integrity:** when later authored scenes are approved, integrate with incident provenance, eligibility predicates, stable dialogue IDs, appropriate speaker history and dedicated narrative RNG. Exact approved words and player-visible prior lines remain unchanged on replay/Undo/Redo.
7. **No mandatory character cameos:** do not make Robert Clive, Hastings, Tipu Sultan, Hamilton or George III into automatically present characters. A proposed rare named historical cameo would need its own chronology and creative approval.
8. **Royal/Parliament governance:** royal involvement and Westminster scenes are institutionally situated, not an extra invented printed-game phase or law.
9. **No quotations yet:** all suggested 'comic mechanisms' are narrative *prompts*, not approved verbatim dialogue. Subsequent authoring should separately label historical quotations vs original fictional speech and obtain editorial approval.
10. **Narrative restraint:** uncommon period colour is more striking than relentless quipping; serious actions may warrant serious prose.
