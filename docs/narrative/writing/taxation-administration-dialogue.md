# Taxation and Administration — Authored Dialogue Catalogue (Collection 02)

**Status:** User-approved creative draft, 24 October 2026.  
**Scope:** Content and eligibility guidance only; no changes to John Company mechanics.  
**Companions:** [Writing style](style-guide.md), [Company conquest](company-conquest-dialogue.md), [Conquest callbacks](company-conquest-callbacks.md).

## Evidence and event vocabulary

A committed **Tax** action is not proof of excessive taxation, a particular sum changing hands, a depleted treasury, famine or a broken promise. Lines about escalating fiscal oppression require an explicit selected unrest reason **excessive taxation**, repeated relevant recorded taxation, or other real evidence as specified for the line. Historical speech can be fictional, but speakers' claims remain opinions rather than authoritative economic facts.

A **failed administration** scene requires a *specific recorded failed administrative action* with an eligible narrative trigger; no generic failure line should be used solely because an action was taken, an order closed, a Tax occurred, or unrest appeared. If the app lacks such an incident type, defer these lines. Never invent a Governor, formal audit, palace audience, petition, deployment, payment, riot, military response, famine or physical collection.

A dialogue line mentioning **riot**, street demonstrations or torches requires those precise facts if delivered by the chronicler, but can be phrased as a character's conjecture or joke only where the dialogue makes that subjective status obvious. In this collection, prefer `taxation-related unrest` as the eligibility predicate and avoid asserting details about its physical manifestation.

**Speaker links:** An eligible local ruler (sovereign, dispossessed or otherwise politically affected) and Company adviser must have a supported relationship to the region and event. Their lines do not prove attendance, firsthand observation or personal executive responsibility. Complete exchanges are fictional narrative scenes, not mechanical negotiations.

## 1. Taxation — the price of good government

**Trigger:** A committed Tax event affecting a Company-controlled region. If the wording presumes the Company has recently improved collection speed, supplied a new fiscal system or made people destitute, require separate supporting facts. All lines below are subjective comic framing, not mechanical account data.

| ID | Eligible voice/context | Expression | Exact authored text |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| CH-T02-01 | Chronicler; Tax in Company-controlled region; administrative satire | Dry | The Company had introduced a more efficient system of taxation. Money that previously took months to collect could now be demanded immediately. |
| AD-T02-01 | Company adviser, vain/self-congratulatory; Tax | Smug | “A most encouraging result. The natives have demonstrated a remarkable willingness to contribute to their own improvement.” |
| RU-T02-01 | Cynical or pragmatic affected ruler; Tax | Sardonic | “The Company has finally discovered something we can agree upon. We both believe my people possess too much money.” |
| RU-T02-02 | Proud, theatrical, or embittered dispossessed ruler; Tax after conquest | Cutting | “Your directors are welcome to my treasury. I only ask that they leave enough for me to purchase it back.” |

**Guardrails:** CH-T02-01 suggests a specific *new* administrative collection method and increased collection speed. Do **not** select from an ordinary Tax incident without an actual recorded reform or authored historical context establishing that premise. Otherwise withhold it; Codex must not weaken the condition. AD-T02-01 is the adviser's rhetorical conceit, not a verified statement of voluntary public support. RU-T02-02 is bitter hyperbole; do not assert that the personal treasury was actually seized.

## 2. Excessive taxation — unrest

**Trigger:** Committed unrest in the region with **excessive taxation** explicitly recorded as its cause. Mere unrest for another cause is insufficient.

| ID | Eligible voice/context | Expression | Exact authored text |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| CH-T02-02 | Chronicler; tax-caused unrest; satire directed at administration | Dry acid | The inhabitants had begun expressing their dissatisfaction with Company administration. Their objections were particularly unreasonable, as the Company had already spent considerable effort explaining why they should be grateful. |
| AD-T02-02 | Oblivious or hard-hearted Company adviser; tax-caused unrest | Uncomprehending | “I cannot understand their hostility. We have asked for nothing that we were not prepared to take.” |
| RU-T02-03 | Angry, morally engaged ruler; explicit tax-caused unrest, with confirmed petitions/soldiers if literal | Furious | “You demanded their money, ignored their petitions, and sent soldiers when they complained. What precisely did you expect them to celebrate?” |
| RU-T02-04 | Calculating or vindictive ruler; tax-caused unrest | Dark, strategic | “Perhaps the Company should increase the taxes again. I find the people become remarkably patriotic when they can no longer afford to eat.” |

**Guardrails:** CH-T02-02 is satirical ironic free indirect commentary; it should **not** become evidence of a specific recorded propaganda effort. RU-T02-03 requires recorded petitions **and** troop response; **defer** until both facts exist, or obtain separately reviewed revised text. RU-T02-04 is a fictional ruler's vicious opinion, not a narrator-confirmed famine; use rarely, never around confirmed civilian mass suffering.

## 3. Administrative failure — passing the blame

**Trigger:** A genuine recorded unsuccessful administrative initiative with a supported incident category. If such mechanics do not exist, **all entries in this section are future content only**.

| ID | Eligible voice/context | Expression | Exact authored text |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| CH-T02-03 | Chronicler; committed failure | Wry | The Company's latest initiative had failed. Fortunately, its administrative structure ensured that responsibility could be distributed so widely that nobody was required to accept any. |
| AD-T02-03 | Defensive bureaucratic adviser; committed failure | Aggrieved | “The policy was perfectly sound. Its implementation was regrettably entrusted to people who attempted to carry it out.” |
| AD-T02-04 | Careerist adviser; failure associated with eligible leadership | Opportunistic | “A most unfortunate affair. I believe the Governor would benefit enormously from my advice, particularly concerning his replacement.” |

**Guardrails:** AD-T02-04 requires a relevant Governor to be a supported in-world figure, or stays deferred. RU-T02-05 requires an actual authored/saved three-person argument and justified witness relationship, not merely an administrative failure. No backfilled unseen meeting.

## 4. Short whole conversational scenes

These are **indivisible authored scenes**. Only one such exchange should be shown normally for an eligible major moment, with optional expansion. A scene may introduce exactly these fictional utterances into the saved conversation history, but not unrecorded game outcomes.

### EX-T02-01 — The benefits of civilization

**Trigger:** Tax after a confirmed Company conquest of the *same* region, with eligible dispossessed ruler and adviser. The ruler's reference to losing wealth is personal rhetoric, not a validated account balance. The exchange needs a plausible shared conversational setting but **must not be presented as a literal eyewitness account**.

1. **Company adviser:** “His Highness must understand that the Company's administration brings considerable benefits.”
2. **Indian ruler:** “Indeed. Before your arrival, I had a kingdom and considerable wealth. Now I have neither, but I am assured the administration is excellent.”
3. **Company adviser:** “Progress inevitably requires sacrifice.”
4. **Indian ruler:** “And how fortunate that you have found someone else to make it.”

**Possible future memory:** Save the exact claim that administration brings benefits and the exact claim about sacrifice; either can be contradicted or echoed later if the speaker knew it.

### EX-T02-02 — A most successful reform

**Trigger:** Explicitly recorded tax-caused unrest, plus **an actually recorded revenue reform** for the literal first line. A generic Tax action does not qualify. “Rioting” is the ruler's colourful characterisation of an unrest event, not an independent declaration of street violence.

1. **Company adviser:** “The revenue reforms have been an unqualified success.”
2. **Indian ruler:** “Then why are the people rioting?”
3. **Company adviser:** “An unfortunate misunderstanding of their benefits.”
4. **Indian ruler:** “Perhaps you should explain the benefits to them personally.”
5. **Company adviser:** “I fear they are not presently disposed to reason.”
6. **Indian ruler:** “Nor, apparently, to taxation.”

**Editorial status:** Approved for tone, **deferred for implementation** until a factual reform prerequisite is represented. Do not substitute an ordinary Tax action for “revenue reforms.”

### EX-T02-03 — The audit

**Trigger:** Repeated committed taxation in a region *and* a genuine recorded audit/account irregularity. Repeated Tax alone does not establish a discovered irregularity.

1. **Company adviser:** “We have discovered certain irregularities in the accounts.”
2. **Indian ruler:** “How distressing. Has someone been keeping some of the money?”
3. **Company adviser:** “That is precisely what we intend to determine.”
4. **Indian ruler:** “Then I suggest you begin in London.”

**Editorial status:** Approved for tone, **deferred for implementation** until there is a supported audit/irregularity incident. Never conjure theft or missing revenue as historical fact.

## 5. Persistent callbacks

All callbacks require real prior committed incidents in the current branch, relevant participants/knowledge, and **saved** attributed speech if the callback depends on someone having said something. An undid-and-abandoned dialogue never happened in the narrative.

| ID | Trigger and required previous evidence | Voice | Exact authored text |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| CB-T02-01 | Adviser previously delivered a saved pro-taxation/reform statement (prefer AD-T02-01 or EX-T02-02 first line), then later **tax-caused unrest** in same region; same adviser | Adviser, rueful | “It appears I may have overestimated their enthusiasm for the reforms.” |
| CB-T02-02 | Same chain as CB-T02-01; the ruler knows this adviser's prior statement and new unrest | Ruler, dry | “Only their enthusiasm? How modest of you.” |
| CB-T02-03 | Earlier Company conquest of this ruler's region, then later committed Tax there | Dispossessed ruler, bitter | “First they took my kingdom. Now they demand that I contribute to its upkeep.” |
| CB-T02-04 | Same adviser previously **saved a statement blaming others** for administrative failure; later separate *actual* administrative failure involves same adviser | Chronicler, dry | The unfortunate official had once again encountered the peculiar difficulty of being responsible for an administration without being responsible for anything it did. |
| CB-T02-05 | Same ruler had **saved an actual warning** that tax-caused unrest would worsen; later **successful rebellion** in the relevant region; warning known and linked | Ruler, vindicated | “I warned them. Of course, they were very busy explaining why I was wrong.” |

**Sequencing and evidence notes:**
- CB-T02-01 requires the earlier statement to genuinely be about a **reform**, rather than just a generic Tax, because its exact wording says “reforms.” If only a boast about collection is saved, this entry is ineligible.
- CB-T02-02 is a direct conversational reply to CB-T02-01; select them as a **paired unit**, never as a standalone disconnected quote.
- CB-T02-03 is emotionally framed, not proof the former ruler has personally paid money.
- CB-T02-04 requires a meaningful position of administrative responsibility and **two distinct failures**, not mere repeated display of the same event.
- CB-T02-05 requires the ruler's earlier warning as an actual saved utterance. The warning cannot be invented retroactively.

## Selection and quality contract

1. Verify event type, cause, scope, affected region, branch and committed order before evaluating personalities.
2. Match individual ruler/adviser voice profiles, current mood and relationships; do not force one stereotypical response on all Indian rulers.
3. Strong satire targets Company hypocrisy and self-exculpation; avoid ethnic ridicule and jokes at the expense of ordinary people's suffering.
4. Save the exact selected line or **complete** exchange, its stable ID, participants, source incident/utterance references and expressions, as part of the unified game/narrative transaction.
5. Vary lines and apply contextual cooldowns. Do not reconstruct dialogue text at display time, alter accepted words without creative review, or insert invented preconditions to increase eligible selections.
6. If required facts are not present in the current incident model, **withhold/defer the line** and allow existing neutral factual fallback. This collection is not permission to add fictional mechanical actions.
7. Future artistic refinement: differentiated historical voices without archaic parody, and more meaningful spoken relationships between recurring individuals.

**Implementation scope:** This writing document can be integrated by Codex only under separate implementation authorisation; it is not a request to edit application code.
