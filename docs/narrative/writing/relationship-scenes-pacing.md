# Relationship Dialogue and Gameplay Pacing — Approved Creative Decision

**Status:** Approved by user, 24 October 2026.  
**Scope:** John Company companion narrative presentation, future authoring and technical selection guidance.  
**Related:** [Writing style](style-guide.md), [Collections 01–05](company-conquest-dialogue.md), [Milestone 2 political memory](../milestone-2-political-memory.md).

## Primary companion experience

The **physical board game remains the centre of play**. Its companion narrative should respond to what actually happened, not interrupt play merely because a relationship scene is available.

**Default scene:** One concise, factual chronicler observation and **one or two reactions** from characters meaningfully related to the event. Some scenes need fewer; sober events can end without a punchline.

**Core interaction:** A game event occurs → select relevant historical/political participants → weight their memories, personalities, relationships and current contexts → display a short, natural reaction → return promptly to play.

Long conversations **must not** become an automatic New Turn ritual or another obligatory phase. The end-of-turn thematic chapter already provides reflection.

## Three levels of narrative density

1. **Event reactions (ordinary):** Chronicler + 1–2 concise lines responding to the current committed outcome. Character voices may include personal relationship history without lengthy exposition.
2. **Personal callbacks (occasional):** One short observation rooted in a prior saved significant event/utterance, used only when relevant to the present event, paced with contextual cooldowns.
3. **Complete conversations (rare and optional):** Carefully authored, indivisible dialogue between established eligible characters. Use sparingly at genuinely significant moments or behind an optional **Read conversation / More perspectives** expansion. Don't force players to read a long scene to continue the physical game.

A full conversation displayed as optional expansion should be generated/selected and saved transactionally at the relevant event, with its historical evidence and speakers, **not invented when someone opens a dossier later**. An unselected scene never happened.

## How to use relationship writing

Approved creative direction for a supplementary **Collection 06 — Between Crowns and Catastrophes**:

- Extract **short, context-aware personal reactions** suitable for the normal event layer.
- Retain the richer parent/heir, ruler/officer, adviser/adviser and elder-ruler exchanges as **rare complete authored scenes** or optional expansion content.
- Embed a remembered line in an end-of-turn chapter or searchable character dossier only when its exact utterance was selected/saved in that campaign.
- Do not fabricate private conversations, advice, rivalry meetings, family memories or quoted statements retrospectively.
- Character depth should be visible chiefly through **changing reactions to actual events**: one line following conquest, another after failure, another after real transfer/death, etc.
- A real relationship can mix hostility, respect and familiarity; grudging affection need not mean political friendship.
- Rarity matters: when a long exchange is exceptional, it can be more memorable and less disruptive.

## Illustrative pacing

**Normal event — failed expedition after prior encounters:**

- **Chronicler:** The Company's latest expedition had failed. Its commander was considerably more surprised than his opponent.
- **Affected ruler:** “Ah, Colonel Pritchard. I had begun to worry that he had finally learned his lesson.”

*Use only with real failed outcome, a genuinely associated officer, repeated encounters, supported in-world knowledge and correct speaker identity; otherwise a factual fallback.*

**Later actual transfer:** “Pritchard has been transferred? How dreadful. I had grown rather accustomed to his invasions.”

**Later actual death, after established rivalry/familiarity:** “He was an infuriating man. I shall miss proving him wrong.”

These are **examples**, not universally eligible facts. Named characters must resolve to the actual campaign cast and transfers/deaths cannot be generated for a joke.

## Deferred expansion: a possible standalone digital game

Longer relationship-driven scenes are **preserved as creative assets and deferred alternatives**, not discarded. If this project someday branches into a standalone single-player political/narrative game unbound by the board game's pace, they may support:

- more frequent personal scenes and character-focused story chapters;
- deeper household, court and Company institutional politics;
- complex relationships, social choices, dynastic stories and character life arcs;
- deliberately interactive, longer exchanges.

This future possibility **does not change the current companion's scope** and is not authorisation to introduce those systems into the board-game app now.

## Integration contract for Codex

Prioritise short event-linked reactions, personal memory callbacks and expandable inspection. Long dialogue scenes remain optional/rare. Implementing presentation logic is a separate technical milestone; publishing this document does not request a code change.

The approved collections and their exact wording remain intact. Future authoring work may propose short-form adaptations of Collection 06, to be reviewed before integration.
