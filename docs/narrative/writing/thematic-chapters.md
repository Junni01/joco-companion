# The Chronicler's Chapters — Collection 08B

**Status:** User-approved creative direction and illustrative chapter writing, 24 October 2026.  
**Scope:** End-of-turn thematic chapters for the board-game companion. This is a writing and evidence contract, **not authorisation to implement application code**.  
**Companions:** [Commerce and the chronicler](commerce-and-chronicles.md), [Style guide](style-guide.md), [Relationship scene pacing](relationship-scenes-pacing.md).

## Purpose and voice

Use **dry political observation with personal development** as the tonal anchor. A chapter should reveal a consequence or changed judgment through a small number of connected, recorded events. The chronicler may be ironic about Company pretensions, especially the gap between conquest, revenue collection and competent government. It should not make civilian suffering, Indian identity or tragic loss the joke.

An ordinary turn gets **at most one or two significant threads**, usually one or two short paragraphs in total. A quiet turn may receive only a factual sentence or no substantial chapter. This is reflection after play, not another compulsory conversation. The full chronological journal remains separate and authoritative. Longer chapters belong to exceptional turns or the final campaign chronicle, not every New Turn.

The examples below are **authored reference passages for the specifically stated hypothetical records**. They are not generic output for any campaign matching a broad event label. Names and regions are illustrative parameters; substitutions must refer to the same actual character and region throughout. No chapter is permitted to invent a promise, completed trade, personal payment, witnessed conversation, motive, causal link or political reconciliation.

## Evidence and composition contract

1. Select material only from **committed structured incidents**, saved dialogue, character-owned memories and interpretations, relationship reasons, arcs and actual political states. Do not derive incidents by parsing instruction text.
2. Build a candidate thread from ordered evidence: earlier context → actual development → current position. Earlier-turn context is allowed, but the chapter must make clear what changed **this turn**. A prior event cannot be retold as if it happened again.
3. Every factual clause must point to the relevant incident/fact IDs. Every personal judgment must be attributable to an eligible character's saved interpretation, relationship reason, memory or exact statement. Distinguish the character's belief from the chronicler's authoritative account.
4. Save the exact selected chapter text, template/version ID, parameter identities and evidence references as part of the same narrative transaction. Rendering, exporting and reopening a dossier must not reroll or rewrite it. Undo/Redo follows the committed branch.
5. Use complete authored passages or explicitly authored conditional variants. Small factual substitutions such as a recorded region/name are acceptable; do not dynamically stitch unsupported sentences into persuasive prose.
6. Verify **end state** and sequence where wording implies them. An opportunity reopened earlier in the turn might have closed again. A deposed ruler must not be called the current ruler. A later reversal may alter the ending of the thread.
7. If a personal or causal predicate is absent, choose a narrower approved variant or neutral factual summary. Do **not** relax the predicate or fill the gap with a plausible-sounding event.
8. Thematic selection should prefer changed fortunes, unresolved contradictions, and genuinely evolving judgments. Avoid restating every event or quoting dialogue simply because it is available. Reuse exact saved quotations sparingly and only with correct speaker attribution.
9. Preserve the board game's vocabulary in physical instructions. Narration may describe a closed/open order as a disrupted/available **commercial opportunity**, but must not claim all markets closed or reopened, trade was completed, or prosperity followed merely from its state.

## CH08-01 — A Kingdom and Its Accounts

**Required sequence:** A recorded Company conquest of the named region; the named Indian ruler occupied the relevant seat at the loss; a later recorded Tax demand and later unsuccessful administration; the ruler retains a sourced conquest grievance. The ruler's unfavorable current judgment after administration must be supported by a saved assessment or eligible relationship reason. The Company still controls the region at chapter end. If these facts span turns, identify the current-turn development correctly.

> The Company had taken Bombay by force. Governing it proved a slower undertaking, although its demands for revenue arrived promptly.
>
> Madhav, who had lost the region to the Company, found little reason to revise his opinion after an unsuccessful attempt at administration. The Company still held Bombay. Its claim to understand the place was proving less secure.

**Factual guard:** “Demands for revenue” means a recorded Tax action, not actual payment or transfer from Madhav. “Claim to understand” is the chronicler's irony about the unsuccessful administration, not a fabricated Company proclamation. If no sourced current personal assessment exists, omit the sentence about Madhav's opinion; do not infer it from the failed action alone. Do not use the final control sentence if the Company later lost Bombay.

**Editorial aim:** Political irony plus a particular person's persistent grievance, without inventing a confrontation or making every defeat a character's automatic emotional transformation.

## CH08-02 — The Opening and Closing of Bombay

**Required sequence:** At least two recorded disruptions and two later restorations of a commercial opportunity in the same region, with the opportunity available at chapter end. The same secretary personally experienced the relevant restorations and has saved earlier hopeful words followed by a later cautious reassessment. The chapter must not transfer that private experience to a replacement appointee.

> A trading opportunity in Bombay was disrupted and restored, then disrupted again. On each occasion the Company recovered access. On each occasion it found that access rather less permanent than its officials had hoped.
>
> Mr. Hale had initially greeted the restoration with confidence. By the turn's end, his announcements had grown more cautious. The commercial opportunity remained available; his faith in its staying that way had not survived the same sequence.

**Factual guard:** Use “its officials had hoped” only if an eligible saved official statement or interpretation supports that hope. Use “announcements” only for that secretary's actual saved lines; otherwise write “his expectations.” If the last opportunity is closed, use a separately authored ending rather than “remained available.” Neither order state nor restoration proves a sale, widespread prosperity, formal treaty or general opening of all markets.

**Editorial aim:** The joke lands on the Company's short institutional memory while the secretary's private experience shows a real, evidenced change in tone.

## CH08-03 — A Useful Protector

**Required sequence:** An actual Commander intervention restored access to a specific commercial opportunity; the named Indian ruler has a sourced earlier Company-conquest grievance and later recorded beneficial encounters or assessments sufficient to support a qualified shift toward cooperation. The saved assessment must still preserve independent objections. Do not infer gratitude from the intervention itself.

> Company soldiers restored access to a commercial opportunity in Bombay. It was not the first time Company power had settled a local difficulty; nor had Madhav forgotten how that power had first arrived.
>
> He had begun to value the protection the Company could offer. That was a change in judgment, not a surrender of his own. Madhav could welcome a useful intervention and still object to the terms on which the Company expected to be thanked.

**Factual guard:** “Not the first time” requires at least one earlier **relevant recorded intervention**; otherwise use an authored first-intervention variant. “Protection,” “value,” and “welcome” require owned beneficial experience and a saved current interpretation, not merely a mechanically reopened order. The concluding expectation of thanks is ironic commentary on an evidenced Company attitude or saved utterance; omit it if unsupported. Soldiers restored an opportunity through an actual action; the passage does not assert that they escorted merchants or guarded a route. Use Madhav's actual political status, if mentioned elsewhere, rather than calling a deposed person the current ruler.

**Editorial aim:** Cooperation without erased grievance or automatic obedience. The same person can find Company power useful and object to its authority.

## CH08-04 — What Remained

**Required sequence:** An actual failed anti-Company rebellion in the named region; Company authority remains afterward; the named participant has a relevant grievance still present in their saved state and an earlier saved hope for a different outcome. If a particular grievance is described as having *caused* the rebellion, that causal link must itself be recorded; this passage makes no such claim.

> The rebellion failed, and Company authority survived the turn. Madhav's grievance survived it as well.
>
> He had allowed himself to hope for a different result. The chronicler could record the defeat; it could not, on that evidence alone, declare the old dispute settled.

**Factual guard:** The named character must actually be linked to this rebellion or own an eligible saved interpretation of it; a regional seat alone is not proof that they led it or fought. Do not imply death, injury, imprisonment, spoken silence, personal payment or a hidden negotiation. If the hope is absent, omit that sentence and use a factual variant.

**Editorial aim:** Give defeat gravity, and let an unresolved personal thread remain open without fabricating a new scene of private grief.

## Additional thread patterns to author later

| Recorded pattern | Possible focus | Key guard |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Conquest → Tax → failed administration | Possession versus governance | Tax is a demand, not proven payment |
| Tax → unrest | Pressure and reaction | Attribute causation only if the source records it |
| Disruption → restoration → later disruption | Fragile commercial access | One order is not the whole regional economy |
| Defeat → saved reassessment | Revised confidence or grievance | Do not generate an attitude change from defeat alone |
| Grievance → repeated useful interventions | Uneasy cooperation | Preserve the grievance and political independence |
| Rebellion → later unresolved grievance | Victory without settlement | Only the actual rebellion outcome is authoritative |

## Acceptance and handoff

For representative multi-turn campaigns, inspect each chosen passage alongside its chronological incidents, source IDs, saved utterances and final political/order states. Review at least one case where an attractive passage is **rejected** because a personal or causal prerequisite is missing. Review repetition across turns: a theme may continue, but the same paragraph should not recur as if nothing changed.

This document approves the **creative direction and reference wording** for 08B. It does not approve implementing 08B, changing application code, or expanding the normal event-dialogue length. Future standalone-game chapters and longer conversations remain deferred alternatives.
