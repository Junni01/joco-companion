# Commerce, Repetition and the Chronicler's Voice — Collection 08

**Status:** User-approved creative direction and dialogue examples, 24 October 2026.  
**Scope:** Narrative writing and pacing specification, not implementation authorisation.  
**Companions:** [Style guide](style-guide.md), [Relationship scene pacing](relationship-scenes-pacing.md), [Fallback polish](fallback-dialogue-polish.md).

## What an order represents

In *John Company 2E*, **orders are economic opportunities** that Company and firm traders can fill using the Trade action. The rules explain that a **closed order represents trade disruption or anti-Company sentiment**. Opening an order makes an opportunity available again, but does **not** prove that every trade route in the region is open, that a merchant has actually completed a sale, or that a named treaty was made. A filled order represents an exploited commercial opportunity producing the game's revenue outcome.

In-game **instructions must continue to use exact component terminology** (open/close/fill order, cascade, which order space). **Fictional dialogue** instead describes the in-world condition.

| Game concept | Preferred fictional vocabulary | Guard |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Open order | Available trading opportunity; commercial access restored | Not a claim all markets reopened |
| Closed order | Interrupted opportunity; trading difficulties; commerce disrupted | Not proof all commerce halted |
| Filled order | Completed trading venture; commercial revenue | Only if filling is actually recorded |
| Cascade | Disruption spreading through connected commercial opportunities | Only if an actual cascade occurred |
| Special Envoy opens order | Diplomacy restored an opportunity | Do not invent treaty/negotiation details |
| Commander opens order | Military intervention restored an opportunity | Do not assume soldiers personally escorted merchants |
| Windfall | Unexpected benefit from actual trading activity | Do not invent its personal recipients |
| Tax | Revenue demands or taxation | Do not invent actual personal payment |
| Unrest | Popular discontent or political instability | Do not equate with a resolved rebellion |

A ruler can welcome trade and resist Company rule; disrupted trade can harm local merchants as well as the Company. Political alignment never requires approval of all Company commercial demands.

## 1. A trading opportunity is disrupted

**Trigger:** Actual committed closure of one or more orders in a region. This is not a claim that commerce throughout the entire region stopped.

### TR08-DISR-CH-01 — Chronicler

> Commercial activity in {region} had suffered another interruption. The Company's merchants were expected to take the development personally.

**Eligibility:** Repeated order closures in a region, or replace “another” through separately approved wording if first closure. The second sentence is ironic inference about merchant interests, not an attributed quotation or a documented response.

### TR08-DISR-SEC-01 — Company secretary, irritated

> “A most unfortunate disruption. Commerce cannot be expected to flourish when everyone insists upon having opinions about it.”

**Eligibility:** Current closure, associated Company secretary, pompous/procedural voice. Comic dismissal of local concerns is the character's opinion.

### TR08-DISR-RUL-01 — Proud ruler

> “The Company appears astonished that trade requires the cooperation of those with whom it wishes to trade.”

**Eligibility:** Actual affected commercial opportunity, proud/critical ruler. It is a sardonic belief, not objective proof of a Company's surprise or a new negotiation.

### TR08-DISR-RUL-02 — Pragmatic ruler

> “The merchants will suffer from this disruption as well. We should be careful not to mistake inconvenience for victory.”

**Eligibility:** Closure and pragmatic ruler whose interests include local trade. The future consequence is a warning, not a verified merchant loss.

## 2. Diplomatic restoration

**Trigger:** Actual Special Envoy action opens a closed order. Never use for military intervention, unrelated Windfall or natural order state.

### TR08-DIPL-CH-01 — Chronicler

> Diplomatic efforts had restored access to a commercial opportunity. The Company was delighted to discover that conversation could occasionally achieve what threats could not.

**Guard:** First sentence is factual if exact diplomatic source exists; second is ironic speculation, not a record of direct talks or threats.

### TR08-DIPL-SEC-01 — Company secretary, pleased

> “A most satisfactory settlement. It is reassuring to discover that mutual understanding remains possible when sufficient profit is involved.”

**Guard:** “Settlement” is the secretary's characterisation of an improved trade position, not a formal treaty.

### TR08-DIPL-RUL-01 — Cautious ruler

> “The merchants may return to business. Whether this improves our relations with the Company remains to be seen.”

**Guard:** “May” expresses a newly accessible opportunity, not proof a merchant filled an order.

### TR08-DIPL-RUL-02 — Sarcastic ruler

> “How extraordinary. The Company has obtained something it wanted without conquering anyone.”

**Guard:** Actual non-conquest diplomacy; no specific negotiation invented.

## 3. Military restoration of trade

**Trigger:** Committed Commander/Army intervention explicitly reopened at least one closed order. Do not use for military conquest, defence with no commercial effect, or merely clearing unrest.

### TR08-MIL-CH-01 — Chronicler

> The Company's soldiers had restored access to trade. Its merchants were once again free to pursue commerce under the reassuring protection of armed men.

**Guard:** “access to trade” refers to the affected opportunity only. The second sentence is satirical generalisation; defer if it would misleadingly claim specific soldiers guard all merchants.

### TR08-MIL-OFF-01 — Company officer, proud

> “The markets are open again. A magnificent demonstration of the benefits of firm government.”

**Guard:** In-world exaggeration by an officer; factual narrator must not say *all* markets reopened.

### TR08-MIL-RUL-01 — Contemptuous ruler

> “I understand the Company has brought soldiers to encourage trade. Its understanding of voluntary commerce continues to fascinate me.”

**Guard:** Requires supported military action reopening opportunity. Ruler's attribution of motives is subjective.

### TR08-MIL-RUL-02 — Practical ruler

> “The trade routes are accessible again. Whether the soldiers have improved anyone's confidence is another matter.”

**Guard:** Restrict to affected reopened connections; avoid claims of every route in the region.

## 4. Repetition is a character experience, not a stuck template

A recurring secretary must not emit the same saved reassurance every time a closed order reopens. The previous multi-turn review showed a secretary repeating an identical “I hope the improvement lasts” announcement eight times. Future selection must consider the *same person's* actual prior successful/restored opportunities and prior saved speeches, with normal cooldowns.

The following are **alternative authored utterances**, not a compulsory sequence. Their meaning depends on the speaker's history and temperament.

### TR08-REST-SEC-01 — First restoration, hopeful

> “Commerce has resumed. I sincerely hope this marks the beginning of a more stable arrangement.”

**Eligibility:** First personally relevant restoration; cautious/hopeful secretary. “Commerce” is shorthand for renewed access in context, not a statement that the entire region's economy resumed.

### TR08-REST-SEC-02 — Repeated restoration, now cautious

> “We have restored commercial access once more. I shall be rather more cautious in predicting how long it will last.”

**Eligibility:** Multiple real restorations known to the same speaker and earlier expectation/hope saved. This new statement does not imply past deliberate deception.

### TR08-REST-SEC-03 — Familiar ritual, exasperated

> “I have prepared the usual proclamation announcing the restoration of trade. Perhaps we might dispense with the ceremony this time.”

**Eligibility:** Several relevant repetitions and an eccentric/procedural voice. “Prepared” is fictional first-person rhetoric, not proof a literal proclamation has been drafted. Do not create a formal proclamation fact.

### TR08-REST-SEC-04 — Long experience, resigned

> “I am beginning to suspect that maintaining our commercial privileges requires considerably more effort than obtaining them.”

**Eligibility:** Multiple disruptions and reopenings supported by the same speaker's history; cautious or weary development. Initial commercial acquisition need not have been witnessed personally, so do not ascribe personal negotiations.

**Continuity rule:** A new appointee may know the institution's factual commercial history but must **not inherit a predecessor's private exasperation or personal utterances** as if their own. Institutional memory and individual experience remain distinct. Repetition metrics must count events/lines on the committed branch, not abandoned Undo alternatives.

## 5. Cascading trade disruption

**Trigger:** A genuine committed cascade that closes connected orders across trading links. Simple repeated closures in one region are not automatically a cascade.

### TR08-CASC-CH-01 — Chronicler

> The disruption spread along the trading network. Commerce, it appeared, possessed a remarkable talent for sharing misfortune.

### TR08-CASC-SEC-01 — Company secretary, alarmed

> “The difficulties have spread to neighbouring markets. I had understood that commercial enterprise was supposed to encourage cooperation.”

### TR08-CASC-RUL-01 — Indian ruler, amused

> “The Company spent years connecting the markets of India. It seems they have finally discovered that trouble can travel along the same roads.”

**Guard:** The statement about Company history is sardonic *ruler opinion*; do not infer the Company literally built roads/trade links or invested over years.

### TR08-CASC-OFF-01 — Company officer, outraged

> “This is intolerable! We have invested considerable effort in establishing these commercial connections, and now they are being used to spread dissatisfaction!”

**Guard:** Requires an affected officer's voice; “we invested” is self-important rhetoric, not independent historical evidence of Company construction.

## 6. Cooperation without automatic gratitude

These are **illustrative phase-dependent reactions**, not a requirement for an artificial three-stage path. Changing political interpretations must have supporting genuine experiences.

### TR08-REL-RUL-01 — Hostile to commercial dominance

> “The Company calls it free trade. I notice that freedom seems to belong almost exclusively to the Company.”

**Guard:** Character criticism, not evidence of a recorded Company “free trade” proclamation.

### TR08-REL-RUL-02 — Pragmatic, mixed outcomes

> “Their merchants have brought prosperity to some and trouble to others. I should prefer to encourage the former without inviting more of the latter.”

**Guard:** Needs actual evidence of mixed commercial effects or saved interpretations; otherwise **defer**, since order states alone do not prove economic prosperity or material deprivation.

### TR08-REL-RUL-03 — Cooperative but independent

> “The Company has proved a useful partner. I intend to remember that partnership is not the same thing as obedience.”

**Guard:** Actual repeated beneficial dealings/derived political cooperation. Does not make the ruler a willing Company subject or alter any mechanical relationship.

The narrative system should distinguish cooperation/protection, taxation resentment, original conquest grievance and personal attitude to particular advisers. A shift toward Company alignment must not make older facts vanish or force every later reaction into praise.

## 7. Thematic chronicler chapters

**Approved approach:** A turn chapter follows one or two important *storylines*, not a transcript of action labels or a fixed chronology recitation. The complete chronological journal is retained separately and remains authoritative.

### Input contract

At chapter creation, select from **committed structured incidents**, significant memories/arcs, real political state transitions, saved speaker statements and relationship reasons. Do not parse instruction prose or display strings to infer events.

Potential threads include:
- control change → tax demands → popular unrest;
- interrupted commercial opportunities → later diplomacy or military restoration;
- an initial ruler grievance → subsequent setbacks or supported reconciliation;
- a costly failed expedition → later attempts;
- a ruler or adviser changing the interpretation of a remembered event.

**No invented facts:** A closed order does not prove all commerce stopped; an opened order does not prove prosperity, commerce completed, formal settlement or merchants' consent. A conquest does not prove a proclamation or promise. A political change does not imply total troop withdrawal or an individual’s return. Character motives are subjective unless previously saved as attitudes.

### Example chapter — *The Price of Prosperity*

The following is a **creative reference** for a hypothetical confirmed event sequence, **not** text to emit verbatim for arbitrary campaigns:

> Bombay's new masters had promised the benefits of commerce, although agreement concerning the price remained elusive.
>
> The Company's demands for revenue had deepened local resentment, while disruptions to trade provided fresh evidence that conquest and administration were rather different occupations.
>
> Military intervention eventually restored access to some commercial opportunities. The Company's officers celebrated the result; Raja Keshav regarded it with considerably less enthusiasm.
>
> His opinion of the Company remained unfavourable, but he had begun to distinguish between its commercial ambitions and the occasional advantages of its protection.

**Evidence warning:** The first paragraph's **promise** requires an actual saved statement; economic benefit, causal resentment, personal reactions and changed attitude require exact prior sources. Without those facts, generate a narrower factually faithful chapter. Do **not** weaken predicates to make a polished example appear.

### Chapter composition rules

1. Identify the turn's most significant themes/connected arcs, with earlier turns as context when relevant.
2. Write a concise beginning → development → current position using **complete authored passage templates** and validated parameters, not runtime AI generation or fabricated filler.
3. Use the chronicler's dry irony; treat tragedy soberly, and reserve sharper satire for administrative absurdity.
4. Use exact saved quotations sparingly when they genuinely occurred and match their speakers. Do not invent retrospective conversations.
5. Prefer **one or two coherent paragraphs** for an ordinary turn; allow a richer concluding chapter for major changes or campaign end.
6. If there is insufficient support for a thematic story, a dignified factual summary or *no substantial chapter* is better than false connecting prose.
7. Keep the complete event journal separately inspectable. The thematic chapter never overwrites it.
8. Chapter output is transactionally saved with sources, exact text and branch-safe Undo/Redo; it must not mutate gameplay.

## Implementation boundaries

- The board-game component vocabulary must remain intact in **physical gameplay instructions** and rule-specific controls. Only fictional narration/dialogue should use in-world trading vocabulary.
- Normal scenes still show the chronicler plus up to two brief relevant reactions; no obligatory long scenes or New Turn conversations.
- Repeated predictions and callbacks should have varied exact text, different attitudes and cooldowns, grounded in real saved history.
- Thematic chapter writing is a separate creative/technical increment from the short-dialogue vocabulary and repetition patch if needed; do not bundle extensive architectural work without approval.
- This document approves **creative direction and writing content**, not application code changes.

## Future alternatives preserved

Longer relationship conversations and more interactive commercial/political storytelling remain possible for the future standalone digital-game branch. They are deferred for the current board-game companion.
