# Military Disasters, Glorious Defeats and Unfortunate Explanations — Authored Dialogue Catalogue (Collection 04)

**Status:** User-approved creative draft, 24 October 2026.  
**Scope:** Fictional campaign dialogue and emergent military rivalries. No new game mechanics.  
**Related:** [Narrative writing style](style-guide.md), [Company conquest](company-conquest-dialogue.md), [Rebellion and unrest](rebellion-unrest-dialogue.md).

## Eligibility and evidence

- A **failed Company expedition** requires a committed failure outcome in the deployment/expedition record; it does not imply a conquest, retreat, prisoner, physical casualty, loss of control or officer death.
- **Catastrophic failure** requires that specific reported outcome, not a colourful synonym for an ordinary failure. Its mechanics do not automatically establish casualties.
- A **victory** requires an actual committed success. An actual defence or unrest-clearing result must not be described as new conquest.
- **Officer participation** means a supported association with an army/presidency/incident; don't falsely identify the adviser as battlefield commander, strategist, physically present or personally responsible.
- A **rivalry** requires persistent recurring identities, directly relevant events and recorded relationship evidence. A political relationship is not proof of actual eyewitness encounters.
- Fictional utterances may be attributed and saved, then remembered; they never establish unsupported troop movements, weather, costs, promises, witnessed proceedings or orders.
- If the event model cannot establish a requirement, hold back the line and use neutral factual fallback. Complete exchanges require a supported in-fiction conversation and must be saved intact.

## 1. The unsuccessful expedition

**Trigger:** Actual committed failed Company military expedition/deployment.

| ID | Speaker/voice | Expression | Exact text |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| CH-M04-01 | Chronicler | Dry | The expedition had been undertaken with every expectation of success. The Company's expectations, unfortunately, had not been consulted by the enemy. |
| AD-M04-01 | Overconfident Company officer | Offended | “Our strategy was flawless. The enemy's refusal to behave as anticipated was both unsporting and militarily irregular.” |
| AD-M04-02 | Bureaucratic Company officer | Evasive | “I should hesitate to call this a defeat. Several important objectives were achieved, including the discovery of objectives that we ought not to have attempted.” |
| AD-M04-03 | Professional Company officer | Sober | “We failed. I suggest we begin with that fact, however unpleasant the gentlemen in London may find it.” |
| RU-M04-01 | Proud ruler affected by expedition | Contemptuous | “It appears that His Majesty's soldiers have discovered the limits of their enthusiasm.” |
| RU-M04-02 | Sardonic ruler affected by expedition | Smiling | “I had been told the Company's army was invincible. I trust someone will inform the army.” |

RU-M04-01's “His Majesty's soldiers” is a fictional character's description; do not establish a false military allegiance. RU-M04-02 refers to an earlier alleged invincibility claim: prefer an actual saved boast, otherwise this line is a character's sardonic allegation, not objective fact.

| ID | Speaker/voice | Expression | Exact text |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| CH-M04-02 | Chronicler | Darkly ironic | The expedition had ended disastrously. Its architects were already preparing explanations, an undertaking for which they appeared considerably better equipped. |
| AD-M04-04 | Careerist Company officer | Self-preserving | “It is essential that London understands the true nature of this affair. I recommend that we describe it as an unexpectedly comprehensive reconnaissance.” |

| RU-M04-03 | Triumphant affected ruler | Proud | “They came to teach us the art of war. I confess I have learned a great deal about how not to practise it.” |

## 4. Complete authored exchanges

These are indivisible units, not assembled line-by-line at runtime. The in-fiction exchange introduces the exact dialogue statements, not a new military or diplomatic event. A character who never encountered the other should not be made to claim firsthand recollection.

### EX-M04-01 — An unfortunate misunderstanding

**Requirements:** Failed Company *attack* (not merely a failed defence) and eligible Company officer and opposing ruler who can reasonably speak as recurring narrative characters.

1. **Company officer:** “The enemy appears to have misunderstood our intentions.”
2. **Indian ruler:** “You arrived with an army.”
3. **Company officer:** “A precautionary measure.”
4. **Indian ruler:** “You attacked.”
5. **Company officer:** “A regrettable escalation.”
6. **Indian ruler:** “You lost.”
7. **Company officer:** “A temporary misunderstanding.”
8. **Indian ruler:** “Then I trust your next explanation will arrive without soldiers.”

**Guard:** A supported armed attack is necessary because it is explicitly asserted. The dialogue does not independently establish an actual in-person parley.

### EX-M04-02 — The difficulties of command

**Requirements:** Actual catastrophic military failure, officer genuinely responsible for selecting subordinates **and** relevant adviser knowledge of it. Without that structured evidence, **defer**.

1. **Company officer:** “My plans were excellent. My subordinates were inadequate.”
2. **Company adviser:** “You selected the subordinates.”
3. **Company officer:** “An unfortunate consequence of excessive confidence in my own judgment.”
4. **Company adviser:** “At last, we have identified the cause.”
5. **Company officer:** “I trust you will omit that observation from your report.”

**Future memory:** An actual saved barb can increase tension between the characters; it needn't mechanically create a report.

### EX-M04-03 — A remarkable military career

**Requirements:** Same recurring Company officer suffered multiple committed defeats; same ruler knows them and an earlier **saved statement** from the officer about an enemy's exceptional fortune, as implied by “last time.”

1. **Company officer:** “I have never encountered an adversary quite so fortunate.”
2. **Indian ruler:** “You said precisely the same thing last time.”
3. **Company officer:** “A remarkable coincidence.”
4. **Indian ruler:** “Indeed. Your defeats are becoming increasingly difficult to distinguish.”
5. **Company officer:** “I shall remind you that I have also enjoyed considerable successes.”
6. **Indian ruler:** “Of course. I understand you are particularly accomplished at returning home.”

**Guard:** Requires actual earlier saved wording substantively matching the ruler's claim, multiple relevant defeats, and appropriate participant knowledge. The officer's boast of other successes can be mere self-aggrandisement, but the chronicler must never present invented successes as fact.

## 6. Rare emerging eccentricities

### EC-M04-01 — An officer who blames the weather

**Prerequisites:** Multiple defeats with genuinely saved/known relevant storms or weather records, growing overconfident denial. Don't imply the weather caused a defeat if that causal link isn't established.

- **After recorded weather and failed expedition:** “I have the greatest respect for our enemy. I should respect him considerably less if he would cease collaborating with the weather.”
- **Later actual success in a recorded storm, same officer with saved earlier line:** “Precisely as I have always maintained: weather is merely another instrument of superior generalship.”

If no storm/weather connection exists, both lines are ineligible. An ironic statement is not weather mechanics.

### EC-M04-02 — A ruler who keeps score

**Prerequisites:** The officer and ruler have an established persistent rivalry and at least three **specific validated encounters** matching the exact result count.

- **After exactly three relevant tracked encounters (two defeats and one documented retreat):** “Three expeditions, two defeats and one most impressive retreat. I begin to think the Colonel regards this as an annual engagement.”
- **Later after a genuine fourth encounter does *not* occur because the officer was transferred or removed, with the prior joke actually saved:** “I had hoped for a fourth encounter. Apparently, London has finally learned to count.”

**Guards:** Retreat requires explicit event support; a failed deployment does not prove retreat. The second line is playful speculation about why London acted, not proof of its actual deliberation.

## 8. Narrative relationship design guidance — not new mechanics

Long-running enemies can develop familiarity and grudging affection **without becoming friends or ceasing to be political rivals**. Track these *distinguishable narrative qualities* as design dimensions or derive them from existing reason-linked memories:

| Quality | Dramatic meaning | Possible provenance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hostility | Personal resentment | Conquests, insults, broken promises, rival actions |
| Respect | Recognition of competence | Repeated effective performance, honourable conduct |
| Familiarity | Knowledge of another's habits | Multiple supported encounters and saved exchanges |
| Humiliation | Personally salient defeats or public verbal reversals | Significant incidents, saved humiliating comments |
| Obsession | Rivalry occupying disproportionate attention | Repeated focus on the same rival over time |

**Technical caution:** These are editorially approved *narrative axes*, not a directive to add five permanent numeric stats. Codex should first determine whether current M2 relationships/memories already support them. Any new storage schema or mechanical effects require separate technical approval.

An adversary may have **high hostility, high familiarity, low respect**, and still express sincere grief after a well-established rival's recorded death. Personality development should remain gradual and experience-dependent; quick moods can change rapidly.

## Whole-scene selection and evidence rules

1. All content uses stable IDs, contextual eligibility, identities, expressions, saved exact speech, citations to prerequisite incidents/utterances, and branch-safe Undo/Redo persistence.
2. Respect scope: army advisers, local rulers and actual regions; don't imply a character fought, witnessed, negotiated or commanded without supporting recorded involvement.
3. Never attribute a promise or recurring catchphrase to someone who didn't say it in saved dialogue.
4. Military failure, catastrophic failure, military victory, rebellion and sovereignty transfer are different conditions. Do not substitute one for another just to increase dialogue frequency.
5. Use the most natural character voice; don't force all officers into buffoonery or all rulers into sarcasm. A professional officer can be blunt, while a vain officer becomes comically grander over time.
6. Keep normal scenes brief (chronicler plus up to two comments or one complete exchange) and other perspectives expandable. A catastrophic or tragic result can end in dignified silence.
7. Approved exact words should not be silently rewritten or their evidence conditions weakened. Ineligible material stays available for later content expansion.
8. Narrative outcomes may shape dramatic relationships and memories but **never override physical game decisions, dice, legal targets or actual outcomes**.
9. Publication here approves writing only. Integration of application code remains a separate step.
