# Crowns, Families and Unfortunate Relatives — Authored Dialogue Catalogue (Collection 05)

**Status:** User-approved creative draft, 24 October 2026.  
**Scope:** Fictional dynastic character writing. This is an authored-content catalogue, **not** authority to add marriages, assassinations, political actions, new rules or automatic ruler fates.  
**Companions:** [Style guide](style-guide.md), [Rebellion and unrest](rebellion-unrest-dialogue.md), [Military disasters](military-disasters-dialogue.md), [Narrative requirements](../narrative-requirements.md).

## Governing principles and deferred activation

Family history creates expectations, **not predetermined personalities**. Descendants may remember the same grievance, respect it, reject it, reinterpret it, or develop a more cautious attitude independently. The chronicler's record of the actual past remains authoritative; character interpretations are explicitly subjective.

Distinguish:
- **Genealogy established:** Parent, child, spouse, cousin, heir, descendant, relative must be backed by an actual generated/saved relationship. Never infer parentage or marital ties from a shared dynasty name.
- **Heir recognised:** The character is genuinely a recognised heir in narrative state. A family member or plausible candidate is not automatically heir.
- **Succession:** A previous ruler actually died or otherwise left office and a successor genuinely took the relevant position; an app notification or New Turn alone does not suffice.
- **Exile:** A real narrative exile state with whereabouts, protector/host and claim where those attributes are mentioned. Mere loss of sovereignty is not exile.
- **Restoration:** A previously deposed/exiled *same character* actually returned to a supported political position. Independence of the region without that person's return is not personal restoration.
- **Personal utterances:** Claims and sentiments may be authored fiction, but are then saved exactly. Do not backfill conversations, private motivations, paternal advice or declarations.
- **Untracked content:** Marriages, rival claimants, cousin intrigues, deaths, restorations, court proceedings and household politics belong to future milestones unless their state and incident contracts exist. **Keep those lines dormant** instead of fabricating event facts.

Age affects life stage and voice when recorded; expressive portraits remain the artistic goal. Do not require ageing-specific portrait variants.

## 3. Complete authored family scenes

All exchanges are **indivisible**, saved as actual selected fictional speech. They do not spawn marriages, attempted murder, councillors, treaties, actual meetings or political outcomes.

### EX-F05-01 — An heir in a hurry

**Eligibility:** Living parent ruler, actual son/heir, established difficult relationship. Both must be available and able to address one another.

1. **Ruler:** “You are young. There is much you have yet to learn.”
2. **Heir:** “And I am fortunate to have such an experienced teacher.”
3. **Ruler:** “Then perhaps you will finally begin listening.”
4. **Heir:** “I listen very carefully, Father. Particularly when you speak of the mistakes you made at my age.”
5. **Ruler:** “I made fewer mistakes than you imagine.”
6. **Heir:** “Naturally. You have had considerably longer to correct the record.”

**Future evidence:** Records what they said, not an independent factual tally of parental mistakes.

### EX-F05-02 — The inheritance

**Eligibility:** New ruler is the child of a predecessor who lost specific territories/revenues to the Company; meaningful prior relationship and appropriate adviser association. The father's fate/status must be established.

1. **Company adviser:** “Your father and the Company enjoyed a long and productive relationship.”
2. **New ruler:** “Indeed. You acquired his territories, his revenues and his patience.”
3. **Company adviser:** “His Highness was always reasonable.”
4. **New ruler:** “Then I fear you may find me a disappointment.”

**Guard:** The adviser's “productive” is subjective. The ruler's “revenues” must not become objective evidence of personal treasury seizure. This scene is ineligible without actual recorded relevant territorial loss; prefer evidence of revenue extraction too.

### EX-F05-03 — The unwelcome relative

**Eligibility:** Actually documented cousins and an established succession rivalry/insecurity; eligible adviser; fictional assertion by ruler, not proof cousin said or did anything unless earlier saved.

1. **Ruler:** “My cousin assures me that he desires nothing but the continued prosperity of our dynasty.”
2. **Adviser:** “A most reassuring sentiment.”
3. **Ruler:** “Indeed. He has been studying the succession so diligently that I begin to fear he expects an examination.”
4. **Adviser:** “Perhaps he is merely conscientious.”
5. **Ruler:** “Then I wish he would be conscientious about something less dependent upon my death.”

**Guard:** For the first and third lines to be literal, the cousin's earlier assurance and attention to succession must be recorded; otherwise **defer**, do not invent them retroactively.

## 5. Exile and restoration — a returning ruler

### EX-F05-04 — The returning king

**Eligibility:** Exactly the same exiled/deposed ruler **actually restored** to a relevant throne; Company adviser has a supported relationship with political outcome; contact/knowledge supported.

1. **Company adviser:** “His Highness's return is a most unexpected development.”
2. **Restored ruler:** “Unexpected by whom?”
3. **Company adviser:** “By those responsible for the present arrangements.”
4. **Restored ruler:** “Then I suggest you inform them that the arrangements have become considerably less present.”

**Guard:** These are personal claims and repartee, not new evidence of secret Company deliberations or formal diplomatic arrangements.

**Whole exchange — The inherited enemy:** CB-F05-02 through CB-F05-05 are a *single four-line authored scene*. They may not be recombined as unrelated templates. For CB-F05-06, the young ruler rejects an inherited feud **without deleting family memories**; the original grievance and older generations' interpretations remain recorded. Distinguish actual hatred from colourful accusation. The descendant may be affectionate, strategically conciliatory, or merely tired of the feud.

## 8. Rare dynastic eccentricities

### EC-F05-01 — A ruler obsessed with his legacy

**Prerequisites:** Ageing ruler with persistent concern about historical reputation after actual repeated setbacks; a recognised descendant where explicitly referenced.

**Complete scene:**
1. **Ruler:** “I have instructed my descendants to remember my victories.”
2. **Adviser:** “And the defeats, Your Highness?”
3. **Ruler:** “Naturally. They should remember whom to blame.”

**Later callback:** Same ruler suffers another actual recorded defeat, original scene selected/saved.
> “I begin to suspect that posterity is becoming unnecessarily well informed.”

The first scene is a fictional declaration, not an actual new will or dynasty-wide instruction.

### EC-F05-02 — An heir who refuses to resemble his father

**Prerequisites:** Heir with recorded family grievance, distinct personal outlook, and actual inheritance/parent link.

> “I honour my father's memory. I merely object to the assumption that honour requires me to repeat all his mistakes.”

**Later callback:** Father really died; had a **saved statement** calling the particular goal/victory impossible; heir now actually succeeds where father failed.
> “He would have called this victory impossible. I wish he had lived to see how wrong he was.”

If father's judgement is not saved, the latter line is ineligible. Family resemblance and personality similarity are never automatic.

## 9. A quiet dynastic ending

**Trigger:** Elderly ruler with long recorded decline, reduced territory and insecure succession.

- **CH-F05-06 — Chronicler, elegiac:** He had spent his life defending a kingdom that existed now chiefly in titles, memories and the stubborn conviction that it might yet be restored.
- **RU-F05-19 — Ruler, quiet:** “When I was young, I thought the kingdom would endure because it had always endured. Now I wonder whether that was the only reason any of us believed it would.”

**Guard:** CH-F05-06 presumes the effective kingdom has nearly vanished; do not use while the region/ruler still clearly controls a substantial intact state. The user's tone requires some scenes without a satirical reply.

## Editorial and Codex integration contract

1. **Future-facing content:** Heir rivalries, exiles/restorations, court plots, personal deaths and dynastic successions may be deferred until represented with proper narrative transactions and states. The presence of these lines is *not* authority to add gameplay mechanics or random deaths.
2. Every candidate has a stable ID, context and evidence prerequisites. Verify ancestry, actual current roles, legal political state and previous statements before selecting.
3. An inherited story is a persistent **personal interpretation** linked to canonical facts, not an alternative authoritative event history. Descendants may change their minds.
4. Store the exact complete selected dialogue, speakers, audience/knowledge, source incident/utterance IDs, expression and eligible branch history. No retroactive phantom conversations.
5. Keep the chronicler factually dependable. Expressions such as “several relatives plotting” need source evidence before they appear as facts; subjective character suspicions remain marked as such.
6. Rare eccentricities emerge gradually from repeated meaningful experience. Avoid uniform theatricality across all rulers. Let doubt and sadness coexist with comedy.
7. Factual prerequisites are hard gates; if missing, use a grounded fallback rather than weakening the approved wording.
8. The companion's physical board game, dice, actions, outcomes, turns and user instructions remain authoritative. Marriages, claims, vows and character motives must never create unrecorded strategic outcomes.
9. This document approves **creative writing only**. Technical implementation requires separate authorisation.
