# Collection 09E — Weather and Rising Tensions: 45 Candidate Scenes

**26 October 2026 · Creative review draft, not authorized production text**

## Contract

Five alternative scenes for each of the five actual saved weather results (25), followed by five alternative scenes for each of the four revealed event counts (20). **These are independent alternative variants, never all displayed together.** Scene dialogue is fictional, not historical quotation. The weather scene is shown first, alongside exact player ship instructions; only after the ship-resolution boundary (or calm-seas navigation) is the event count revealed and the second scene shown. The factual UI displays exact seas and count. Characters and chronicler never talk about the storm die, dice, tiles, cards, event count, game tokens, towers or Elephant.

Storm scenes know which seas experience storms, **not what happened to any particular ship, cargo, port, person, or journey**. Characters may worry, joke, speculate or recount ordinary fictional social behaviour, but must not assert a specific verified casualty, loss, shipwreck, cargo destruction, insurer payout, actual delay or successful voyage. The same caution applies to calm weather: it isn't proof that every ship made port. Count scenes convey a general atmosphere; **the count is known to the UI, not prophetically to any character**. The weather does not canonically cause the political developments. Mix dry comedy, serious anxiety, conflicted opinions and brief incidental voices. Ordinary clerks, mariners and merchants are **nonpersistent**, unnamed fictional voices unless separately approved.

Selection should be deterministic and persisted when integrated; never consume gameplay RNG or pick again on dialog revisit. Use variation/cooldowns to avoid repeats. For early implementation these entries require an editorial approval step.

## Weather: East Indian Ocean — 5 alternatives

### E-01 · Insurance
**Chronicler:** Storms disturbed the eastern seas, and the merchants' thoughts turned to the many uncertainties separating a cargo from its purchaser.

**Merchant:** "I have every confidence in my captain. I have somewhat less confidence in the sea."

**Clerk:** "Shall I inform the insurers?"

**Merchant:** "Inform them of nothing until we have established what they already know."

### E-02 · Dispatches
**Chronicler:** The eastern shipping routes had become a subject of anxious correspondence. Every office wanted news; few were eager to be responsible for delivering it.

**Company secretary:** "The Directors require an accurate report."

**Junior clerk:** "Of the weather, sir?"

**Company secretary:** "Of its consequences. Preferably before there have been any."

### E-03 · A quieter voice
**Chronicler:** Beyond the eastern harbours the weather was unsettled. Onshore, families whose livelihoods depended upon distant voyages watched the horizon with a concern no ledger could adequately describe.

### E-04 · Unsolicited expertise
**Chronicler:** Reports of rough weather in the eastern waters brought out a remarkable number of experts who had never been to sea.

**Company official:** "In my judgment, the captains ought to exercise more prudence."

**Sailor:** "And in mine, sir, the sea ought to consult you before forming an opinion."

### E-05 · Divided interests
**Chronicler:** Storms across the eastern approaches prompted anxious speculation among traders.

**Merchant:** "What concerns me is the safety of the crews."

**Associate:** "A generous sentiment."

**Merchant:** "And naturally the cargoes. I should not wish either concern to be overlooked."

## Weather: West Indian Ocean — 5 alternatives

### W-01 · Western approaches
**Chronicler:** Rough weather troubled the western approaches to India. At Bombay, speculation about the sea proved even less reliable than speculation about the markets.

**Merchant:** "Does anyone have trustworthy news?"

**Clerk:** "Certainly. Unfortunately, it is yesterday's."

### W-02 · The captain's judgment
**Chronicler:** The western seas tested the patience of those who made their living afloat and those who expected prompt returns from dry land.

**Ship's captain:** "My employers would have me defeat the wind by force of character."

**Friend:** "And can you?"

**Ship's captain:** "Not without first promoting the wind to a Company office."

### W-03 · Harbour anxieties
**Chronicler:** Along the western coast, talk of storms left merchants waiting for word from sea. Concern was shared widely, though those with loved ones aboard thought rather differently about the stakes from those awaiting a profit.

### W-04 · Commercial optimism
**Chronicler:** Bad weather in the western waters did little to dampen the confidence of certain financiers.

**Investor:** "One must take a long view of maritime risk."

**Merchant:** "You sound remarkably composed."

**Investor:** "My money is in the warehouses."

### W-05 · Official reassurance
**Chronicler:** Unsettled weather to the west was met by official assurances that the Company's commercial prospects remained sound.

**Junior clerk:** "Shall I add that the situation is entirely under control?"

**Senior clerk:** "No. The sea may object to our presumption."

## Weather: South Indian Ocean — 5 alternatives

### S-01 · Correspondence
**Chronicler:** Storms gathered over the southern waters, and every fresh rumour sent anxious correspondents back to their desks.

**Company secretary:** "The Directors demand an explanation."

**Captain:** "You may tell them the winds have been disagreeable."

**Company secretary:** "I fear they will require someone to blame."

### S-02 · Fishermen and fortunes
**Chronicler:** Along the southern coast, the same troubling weather meant different things to different households. Some worried for family at sea; others watched the market price of anything expected by ship.

### S-03 · The proposed remedy
**Chronicler:** Word of storms in the south prompted an emergency discussion among Company servants.

**Official:** "We must do something."

**Clerk:** "What do you propose?"

**Official:** "First, a committee to establish whether something can be done."

### S-04 · Dangerous confidence
**Chronicler:** The southern seas refused to oblige the merchants' schedules.

**Trader:** "Our arrangements were perfectly sound."

**Sailor:** "An admirable achievement, sir. I shall ask the weather to consider them."

### S-05 · A sober dispatch
**Chronicler:** The southern waters had turned difficult. No reliable account yet settled the fate of particular voyages, and sensible observers withheld both celebrations and obituaries.

## Weather: All three seas — 5 alternatives

### A-01 · Complaints from every quarter
**Chronicler:** Storms troubled the western, southern and eastern seas alike. The Company's correspondence filled with anxious inquiries and competing claims of urgency.

**Senior clerk:** "We have three offices demanding immediate attention."

**Junior clerk:** "Which should we answer first?"

**Senior clerk:** "The one whose correspondent knows the most Directors."

### A-02 · The grand explanation
**Chronicler:** Disturbing weather across India's surrounding seas presented the merchants with difficulties so widespread that no single port could claim exceptional misfortune.

**Director:** "Has anyone prepared a comprehensive explanation?"

**Secretary:** "Yes, sir. It begins, 'Weather is a subject beyond the Company's present competence.'"

### A-03 · Human cost, still unknown
**Chronicler:** From one coast to another, families and trading houses waited for news from the sea. Rumour travelled quickly; trustworthy knowledge was slower, and the distinction mattered most to those with someone aboard.

### A-04 · Parliamentary inquiry
**Chronicler:** Reports of storms across the maritime routes gave rise to stern conversation among London investors.

**Proprietor:** "The Directors cannot be held accountable for the weather."

**Another proprietor:** "Of course not. I merely wish to know when they propose to improve it."

### A-05 · Conflicting reports
**Chronicler:** An unsettled sea surrounded India on every approach, and the Company's offices strained to distinguish observation from conjecture.

**Junior clerk:** "Our correspondents disagree about the severity."

**Senior clerk:** "Then circulate the mildest account to the Directors and keep the others for our own use."

## Weather: No storms — 5 alternatives

### C-01 · Confidence
**Chronicler:** The seas appeared unusually accommodating. Merchants greeted the absence of storms with that peculiar confidence which frequently accompanies good fortune.

**Merchant:** "At last, the heavens favour commerce."

**Clerk:** "Shall I put that in the accounts?"

**Merchant:** "Not until the money arrives."

### C-02 · The quiet watch
**Chronicler:** There was little sign of dangerous weather along the Company's maritime approaches. Sailors made their preparations as carefully as ever; a calm sea was welcome, not a guarantee.

### C-03 · A surplus of advice
**Chronicler:** With the seas comparatively tranquil, certain Company officials turned their attention to improving the conduct of mariners.

**Captain:** "Now that the weather is favourable, they have suggestions for every part of the voyage."

**First mate:** "Perhaps we should request a storm to occupy them."

### C-04 · Premature celebration
**Chronicler:** Gentle conditions upon the seas encouraged optimism in the Company's trading houses.

**Investor:** "A fine prospect for our affairs."

**Merchant:** "The prospect is fine. The accounts have yet to agree."

### C-05 · A little peace
**Chronicler:** The surrounding waters were calm, and in the ports there was, for once, less reason to curse the wind. Those whose loved ones were at sea permitted themselves a little hope.

### T1-01 · Cautious quiet
**Chronicler:** India appeared, for the moment, comparatively settled. The Company's correspondents nonetheless found sufficient uncertainty to discourage anyone from declaring the matter closed.

**Adviser:** "I should not call this alarming."

**Clerk:** "May I call it reassuring?"

**Chronicler:** Many courts went about their business with studied confidence, though an anxious dispatch could still disturb an entire committee in London.

**Secretary:** "The letter is deeply concerned."

**Director:** "About what?"

**Secretary:** "Its author has not yet decided."

### T1-03 · Low, serious note
**Chronicler:** The season opened with fewer outward signs of disorder than some had feared. Experienced observers knew that a quiet court might still conceal an argument of considerable consequence.

**Merchant:** "It is a relief to worry about ordinary things."

**Chronicler:** The political horizon looked almost orderly, which tempted some observers into pronouncements they might later regret.

**Adviser:** "I see no reason for panic."

**Colleague:** "Shall I record that?"

**Adviser:** "Not in my name."

## Editorial QA before Codex integration

- **Count integrity:** Each actual storm-face family has five weather scenes; event-count values 1, 2, 3 and 4 have five tension alternatives each = **45 total**.
- **No hidden-face knowledge:** Event-count scenes must not certify specific future tile contents or locations. They convey an intensity of concern, not the number of incidents *known by eighteenth-century speakers*.
- **No weather casualty claims:** Weather text may depict rumours, concern and nonspecific social activity; it cannot assert a confirmed lost ship, delayed specific arrival, named dead mariner, sunk cargo or actual affected Company treasury.
- **No mechanical language in speech:** All 45 scenes translate player-facing storm and event-count metadata into fictional life, in line with `docs/narrative/component-to-fiction-glossary.md`.
- **No forced causation:** Weather result and event count derive from the same die, but storms do not in-fiction cause or explain the political events. A minor dry joke may contrast the two scenes after separate reveal, but not claim weather changes politics.
- **Pacing:** Only one weather alternative and one tension alternative per phase; single-speaker and multi-speaker scenes both represented. Avoid duplicating a similar bureaucratic joke in the two consecutively selected alternatives; enforce diversity in future selection.
- **No untracked institutions:** Incidental Company employees or merchants in a scene are not a player-owned Writer, a specific Board officeholder or a persistent new NPC.
- **Future scope:** Context-specific regional risks, public discard/pile forecasts, female shareholder VIPs, dynastic events, foreign invasions and royal audiences are separate collections. Do not fabricate these into phase opening.
