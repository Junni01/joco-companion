# Collection 09E — Invasion announcements and force-scale dialogue

**26 October 2026 · 36 candidate scenes · Creative writing approved by user on 26 October 2026; not yet integrated**

## Contract and selection boundary

The 09D-2 weather implementation silently saves the **invasion-targeting storm result**, announces the **actual target regions**, then silently saves **one numeric raw force result (1–6) per target at that target's resolution point**. Exact numeric physical attack strength is separately shown in player instructions. Normal mode retains graphical dice. This dialogue collection supplies prose candidates for two different moments:

1. **Threat announcement:** After all actual target regions are determined, but **before any unrolled future target's strength is known**. The speaker knows the confirmed threatened region(s), but not the outcome, precise numbers, casualties or later actions. Generic Foreign Invasion must **not** be named as French.
2. **Individual force appraisal:** After that target's raw strength has been generated/saved, before players report its defence. These are subjective descriptions of apparent expedition size/seriousness, **not troop counts or guaranteed outcome**. The defender's effective final attack strength may differ due to other rules, so the raw 1–6 bands are **editorial tone selectors only**, not additional mechanics.

Only one announcement scene per attack set and one appraisal per target, with variation, appropriate persistent character eligibility and cooldowns. These 36 are alternatives, not a sequential script. `{region}`, `{regions}`, `{priorRegion}` and `{presidency}` are runtime-safe interpolation placeholders supplied **only** from already committed/known authoritative facts. No arbitrary untrusted prose interpolation. In dialogue, never mention game dice, Elephant, tower, numeric force, deck, cards or event count. Don't describe cities captured, leaders dead, ships lost, a surrender, proven foreign national affiliation, or a surviving French garrison before they are actually established by subsequent committed results.

**Source distinction:** For `foreign`, the invader is simply a **foreign host/expedition**. For `france`, War Against France has actually failed and relevant attacks follow; only this branch may refer to **French interests/forces**, while avoiding newly invented commanders, precise origin, named battles or a permanent French-held province. `france` with **no invasion targets** gets its own short aftermath and must not use any announcement of a real attack.

**Speaker roles:** The chronicler is the reliable frame. A local ruler speaks only if the particular actual identified character is eligible/alive and can plausibly receive the news. Otherwise select an **unnamed local courtier, port official, Company secretary or military adviser**. Actual Company Presidency officers/family identities are not fabricated. Future French or foreign VIP lines are separate gated entries; none of the generic texts below attributes events to a named VIP.

## A. Generic Foreign Invasion: actual announced targets (12)

### F-01 · Single target · First alarm
**Chronicler:** Word of a foreign expedition brought apprehension to {region}. The first reports were sufficiently serious to demand attention, and sufficiently incomplete to invite argument.

**Local courtier:** "Do we know their intentions?"

**Another courtier:** "Only that they have not troubled themselves to seek our permission."

### F-02 · Single target · Company dispatch
**Chronicler:** {region} had become the subject of increasingly urgent military correspondence.

**Company secretary:** "A foreign force threatens the province."

**Senior adviser:** "An unfortunate development. I was hoping to spend this morning being mistaken about something less expensive."

### F-03 · Single target · Proud court
**Chronicler:** News of an approaching foreign force reached the court of {region}.

**Local courtier:** "Shall I inform His Highness that there may be trouble?"

**Second courtier:** "Inform him that there is trouble. Leave 'may be' for the servants who have to explain it."

### F-04 · Single target · Sober
**Chronicler:** Foreign forces threatened {region}. Their purpose and immediate prospects remained uncertain, but the danger to the region could no longer be dismissed as a rumour.

### F-05 · Single target · Merchant calculation
**Chronicler:** Word of foreign military preparations unsettled the merchants of {region}, who preferred their uncertainties commercial rather than martial.

**Merchant:** "Can we continue to do business?"

**Associate:** "Certainly. The question is with whom, and for how long."

### F-06 · Single target · Astonished authority
**Chronicler:** Officials in {region} had barely agreed upon the proper description of the threat when new reports demanded action.

**Local official:** "I propose we issue a firm warning."

**Assistant:** "To the enemy?"

**Local official:** "To anyone who has failed to warn us."

### F-07 · Multiple targets · Several fronts
**Chronicler:** Reports of foreign military activity arrived from several quarters: {regions}. The separate warnings troubled officials who had been counting on each province to remain someone else's concern.

**Company adviser:** "We cannot attend to every danger with equal vigour."

**Secretary:** "Shall I inform the others that they have been assigned a lesser priority?"

### F-08 · Multiple targets · Strategic sobriety
**Chronicler:** The threat was no longer confined to one province. {regions} faced foreign incursions, placing difficult demands on those responsible for their defence.

### F-09 · Multiple targets · Correspondence
**Chronicler:** Alarming dispatches concerning {regions} arrived in quick succession.

**Junior clerk:** "Should I separate the reports by province?"

**Senior clerk:** "Yes. It will make it easier for each governor to explain why the others were to blame."

### F-10 · Multiple targets · Debating priorities
**Chronicler:** The danger to {regions} gave rise to an urgent discussion about priorities.

**Military adviser:** "We must judge each threat on its merits."

**Company official:** "And if they all have merits?"

**Military adviser:** "Then we are presented with an exceptionally unwelcome abundance."

### F-11 · Multiple targets · Courts take notice
**Chronicler:** The arrival of foreign forces near {regions} drew the attention of neighbouring courts, where public confidence and private preparations advanced at markedly different speeds.

### F-12 · Multiple targets · A merchant's map
**Chronicler:** Merchants with interests in {regions} found their prospects increasingly entangled with matters of war.

**Merchant:** "I once considered geographical diversification a safeguard."

**Associate:** "And now?"

**Merchant:** "Now I have learned to worry in several places at once."

## B. War Against France: failed parliamentary outcome and ensuing threats (8)

### FR-01 · Single target · National humiliation
**Chronicler:** The quarrel with France, so readily discussed at a distance, had acquired an immediate Indian consequence. {region} now faced the threat of French military action.

**Company adviser:** "The Directors will wish to know how this became possible."

**Secretary:** "Would they prefer an accurate explanation?"

### FR-02 · Single target · The continental rival
**Chronicler:** News of French forces threatening {region} brought the European rivalry into sharp focus in India.

**Company officer:** "Their ambitions grow more tiresome with every dispatch."

**Companion:** "And ours?"

**Company officer:** "Ours are a matter of national duty."

### FR-03 · Single target · A sober warning
**Chronicler:** French military pressure now threatened {region}. The arguments in London had ended; in India, the consequences had only begun.

### FR-04 · Single target · Rival commerce
**Chronicler:** The possibility of French success near {region} alarmed those who considered the Company's dominance a permanent fixture.

**Merchant:** "I had been assured our position was secure."

**Company official:** "It remains secure in principle."

**Merchant:** "An admirable location for it. Pity our business is conducted elsewhere."

### FR-05 · Multiple targets · London promises
**Chronicler:** French threats developed against {regions}, a turn of affairs unlikely to improve the Company's standing in London.

**Company adviser:** "Our ministers have promised a decisive answer."

**Junior secretary:** "Do they know what the question is, sir?"

### FR-06 · Multiple targets · No spare explanation
**Chronicler:** The struggle with France threatened more than one Indian province: {regions}. Those entrusted with defending the Company's interests found their explanations multiplying almost as quickly as their concerns.

### FR-07 · Multiple targets · British indignation
**Chronicler:** French military activity menaced {regions} and stirred fierce indignation among Company servants.

**Senior official:** "This is an intolerable provocation."

**Junior clerk:** "Shall I say so in our dispatch?"

**Senior official:** "Certainly. Use the strongest language that cannot oblige us to do anything."

### FR-08 · Multiple targets · Merchant priorities
**Chronicler:** Reports of French forces threatening {regions} produced a familiar disagreement.

**Company officer:** "We must defend the honour of Britain."

**Merchant:** "Certainly. Might we defend my warehouses on the way?"

## C. Each target's saved raw invading force: low (1–2), medium (3–4), high (5–6) — 15

**Editorial caveat:** 'Small', 'substantial' and 'formidable' describe a **relative visible impression** keyed to the saved raw 1–6 roll; they are not asserted precise troop numbers, the full modified defence requirement, guarantees of military performance, or news of victory.

### L-01 · Low · Caution
**Chronicler:** The approaching force at {region} appeared limited in scale. Yet those charged with defence were reluctant to mistake a modest expedition for a harmless one.

### L-02 · Low · A boast too early
**Local courtier:** "Their numbers appear unimpressive."

**Experienced adviser:** "Then I recommend we preserve that advantage by taking them seriously."

### L-03 · Low · A short supply of certainty
**Chronicler:** Early reports suggested a comparatively small foreign force near {region}. Opinions differed on whether this made the threat manageable or merely difficult to understand.

### L-04 · Low · The paperwork
**Company clerk:** "The danger seems modest, sir."

**Adviser:** "Then perhaps we shall have the luxury of preparing a sensible response."

**Company clerk:** "Shall I mark the file 'minor'?"

**Adviser:** "I should like to survive long enough to regret that."

### L-05 · Low · Not a demonstration
**Chronicler:** The invaders approaching {region} did not appear to command a great host. They were nevertheless an armed force, and armed forces rarely trouble themselves to become welcome guests.

### M-01 · Medium · A serious expedition
**Chronicler:** The forces approaching {region} appeared substantial enough to demand a considered defence. No one responsible for the province could safely dismiss them as a passing inconvenience.

### M-02 · Medium · Council opinion
**Local courtier:** "The invaders are neither few nor beyond number."

**Military adviser:** "An excellent description. I shall endeavour to devise a defence somewhere between confidence and despair."

### M-03 · Medium · Confidence under pressure
**Chronicler:** A significant foreign force threatened {region}. The court's public assurances remained firm, though the discussion behind closed doors had grown considerably more detailed.

### M-04 · Medium · Competing reports
**Company secretary:** "One report calls the enemy formidable; another says merely substantial."

**Adviser:** "Then arrange for the authors to defend the province until they agree."

### M-05 · Medium · Concern without surrender
**Chronicler:** The foreign expedition assembled against {region} was not one to be brushed aside. Its eventual success remained uncertain, as did the wisdom of several confident pronouncements made in advance.

### H-01 · High · The scale of the threat
**Chronicler:** The force threatening {region} appeared formidable. Those responsible for its defence faced a prospect that demanded more than routine assurances.

### H-02 · High · Official understatement
**Company secretary:** "The reports describe a very considerable enemy."

**Senior adviser:** "Then amend our previous assessment."

**Company secretary:** "Which part, sir?"

**Senior adviser:** "The reassuring part."

### H-03 · High · A ruler's resolve
**Local courtier:** "The invaders have brought great strength against us."

**Experienced counsellor:** "Then His Highness will need courage, prudence and fewer courtiers telling him only what he wishes to hear."

### H-04 · High · Sober observation
**Chronicler:** Reports from {region} spoke of a powerful invading expedition. Its arrival could not decide the outcome by itself, but it sharply narrowed the range of comfortable assumptions.

### H-05 · High · A difficult letter
**Company adviser:** "We must inform the Directors that a formidable force threatens {region}."

**Junior clerk:** "Shall I describe our prospects as uncertain?"

**Company adviser:** "Yes. For once, a cautious word may prove entirely accurate."

## D. No target for failed War Against France — 1

### N-01 · No invasion
**Chronicler:** The confrontation with France brought fresh anxiety to the Company's Indian establishments, but no new invasion developed from the immediate crisis. In London, this distinction was certain to improve the arguments more than the relationships.

**Company secretary:** "Shall I report that no attack has materialised?"

**Senior official:** "By all means. Try not to sound surprised."

## Editorial and engineering handoff notes

1. **Counts:** 12 generic announcements + 8 French announcements + 15 force appraisals + 1 French no-target aftermath = **36 candidates**.
2. **Target specificity:** Single-target lines require a known real `{region}`. Multi-target lines require `{regions}` rendered as actual confirmed targets in legal order. When the named region is not Company-controlled, do **not** attribute dialogue to a Company's Governor; use role-neutral speakers or an eligible local courtier. Only living/actually present persistent ruler voices may use their named identity.
3. **Do not force Commander identity:** Generic Foreign Invasion does not reveal whether forces are Persian, Afghan, French or another specific group; no VIP is implicitly present. French-labeled entries require `type:'france'` and the appropriate actually failed War Against France action. Future conditional French/foreign VIP scenes should reuse stable identity and provenance; they must not be invented by this collection.
4. **Check actual force selector inputs:** 1–2 / 3–4 / 5–6 are **writing selection bands for the saved raw die**, not official game classifications. The player-facing exact final attack strength is computed separately using the published modifier rules. Do not create or store new rule variables based on this wording.
5. **Knowability & timing:** Announce only actual target regions after the target determination; never all future per-region rolls. Do not show a scale line on the announcement page before that target's saved roll. Do not assert defeat, conquest, settlement, peace or survival until the respective report commits.
6. **Long-term history:** A French foothold and recurring commander only become plausible **after an actual compatible successful outcome and the explicit later VIP design**. These threat-stage lines do not create French occupation, a garrison, new control, a rebel ruler or another future guarantee.
7. **Pacing:** Announcements and assessments may be adjacent and must feel distinct; choose a single short scale reaction per target and use different speaker rhythms for multiple-target attacks. No additional mandatory click solely to read prose: place a separate read-only scene within the existing protected narrative dialog architecture.
8. **Review status:** These entries are **creatively approved**, but integration and code changes need a separate authorized Codex task. Do not silently turn them into production speech or rewrite previously accepted quotations.
