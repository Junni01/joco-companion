# Rebellion, Revenge and Unrest — Authored Dialogue Catalogue (Collection 03)

**Status:** User-approved creative draft, 24 October 2026.  
**Scope:** Authored fictional narrative, not new John Company mechanics.  
**Companions:** [Style guide](style-guide.md), [Company conquest](company-conquest-dialogue.md), [Taxation and administration](taxation-administration-dialogue.md), [Conquest callbacks](company-conquest-callbacks.md).

## Fundamental incident distinctions

- **Unrest increased** is not itself a rebellion, a battle, a demonstration, a riot, independence or military violence.
- **Successful rebellion** needs a genuinely committed game event resolving a rebellion and the region's actual changed political control. Mere unrest, a successful Indian conquest elsewhere, or an unconfirmed instruction is insufficient.
- **Failed rebellion** needs a genuinely committed failed rebellion outcome. Do not interpret every failed deployment or a failed uprising elsewhere as a rebellion here.
- **Company loss** does not automatically mean that the entire Company army physically departed, a ruler personally returned to office, or an earlier subject regained their previous throne. Match the relevant person's political status.
- A character's accusation, prediction or rhetorical flourish is **subjective speech**, not a new fact of history. The chronicler must remain grounded in the actual event record.
- A relationship to the affected region does not make a speaker an eyewitness or commander. No unrecorded executions, reprisals, direct negotiations, promises, rebellions, capitulations or troop movements may be asserted as objective events.
- Where the present app does not expose an applicable resolved-rebellion event, **defer** that material instead of mapping it to ordinary unrest or Indian conquest. Neutral factual fallback remains available.

## 1. First signs of rebellion — unrest

**Trigger:** A committed increase in regional unrest (not necessarily rebellion). Wording concerning expected Company gratitude is sardonic commentary; it is not evidence of issued propaganda.

| ID | Voice and conditions | Expression | Exact authored text |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| CH-R03-01 | Chronicler; Company-held region; new unrest | Dry satire | The Company had expected gratitude, or at least obedience. The inhabitants, demonstrating their usual difficulty with administrative terminology, appeared to have confused both with resentment. |
| AD-R03-01 | Proud, oblivious or outraged Company adviser; unrest under Company control | Outraged | “Rebellion? Against us? But we have brought them peace, prosperity and the finest administrative procedures in the world!” |
| RU-R03-01 | Calculating ruler politically affected by unrest | Calculating | “A discontented population is a dangerous thing. Particularly when those responsible for its discontent believe themselves universally adored.” |
| RU-R03-02 | Cautious or pragmatic affected ruler | Restrained | “Anger is easily gathered. It is rather more difficult to persuade it to march in the same direction.” |
| RU-R03-03 | Vindictive ruler after prior committed Company conquest of their region | Cold | “They mistook my silence for surrender. I am beginning to think they may regret the distinction.” |

**Guards:** AD-R03-01 uses “rebellion” as an outraged adviser's *premature characterisation*, never a narrator fact. RU-R03-03 is ineligible if the ruler previously made an explicit saved surrender statement inconsistent with “silence”; nor may it establish an impending rebellion as a gameplay outcome.

**Trigger:** Confirmed successful rebellion in the relevant region, restoring independence or the appropriate sovereignty state. Do not use solely for unrest, order changes or failed Company defence.

## 3. Failed rebellion

**Trigger:** An *actually resolved* failed rebellion in the region. Default tone more sober than for administrative setbacks.

| ID | Voice and conditions | Expression | Exact authored text |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| CH-R03-03 | Chronicler; recorded failed rebellion | Sober | The rebellion had failed. Its causes, being considerably more difficult to defeat than its participants, remained. |
| RU-R03-07 | Defiant ruler tied to failed rebellion | Defiant | “They have defeated us again. I had hoped for a different ending, but I never promised them an easy one.” |
| RU-R03-08 | Bitter ruler with prior actual restoration, then **second** loss of sovereignty | Bitter | “I have lost my kingdom twice now. I begin to suspect the Company intends to make a habit of returning it to me only so that it may take it away again.” |
| RU-R03-09 | Weary ruler who earlier explicitly supported resistance in saved speech | Ashamed | “I asked them to believe that things could change. I wonder how many will believe me again.” |
| AD-R03-04 | Smug Company adviser; failed rebellion suppressed | Smug | “Order has been restored. I trust we shall not be troubled by further expressions of political enthusiasm.” |
| AD-R03-05 | Professional/sober Company adviser; same event | Sober | “We have suppressed the rebellion. That does not mean we have settled the matter.” |

**Guards:** RU-R03-07 needs relevant prior defeat for “again”; if this is the first, hold it back. RU-R03-08 requires *two concrete losses* separated by *genuine restoration*; merely two failed attacks cannot qualify. RU-R03-09 is not evidence the ruler personally organised the uprising: it requires an earlier saved exhortation. CH-R03-03 asserts that grievances remain; select only if the associated grievance/unrest is recorded or a political grievance still persists.

## 4. Complete authored conversational scenes

Each exchange is one indivisible scene. Save participants, exact text, audience/knowledge, chosen expression and evidence; do not splice separate replies together. A fictional exchange does not create a meeting, treaty or military incident in the game rules.

### EX-R03-01 — The benefits of British rule

**Requirements:** Actual successful rebellion after prior Company conquest; eligible ruler and Company adviser; independent region; context supports this pair speaking. Dialogue is fictional rhetorical conversation, not a negotiated settlement.

1. **Company adviser:** “His Highness cannot seriously believe that his people will be better off without the Company's protection.”
2. **Indian ruler:** “I cannot say. We have had so little opportunity to conduct the experiment.”
3. **Company adviser:** “You are making a very dangerous mistake.”
4. **Indian ruler:** “Then I trust you will permit me the same privilege your Company has enjoyed so frequently.”

**Future evidence:** The adviser's utterance that this is a dangerous mistake is a *saved personal assertion*, not an objective prediction.

### EX-R03-02 — A temporary setback

**Requirements:** Recorded failed rebellion, affected local ruler and relevant Company adviser; voice profile supports understated ruler determination.

1. **Company adviser:** “I trust this unfortunate episode has taught His Highness a valuable lesson.”
2. **Indian ruler:** “Several.”
3. **Company adviser:** “I am delighted to hear it.”
4. **Indian ruler:** “Most concern the Company's weaknesses. One concerns my own.”

**Future evidence:** A lesson about weakness is an utterance, not a newly known Company military vulnerability or stat.

### EX-R03-03 — The loyal subjects

**Requirements:** Actual unrest in a Company-held region; eligible ruler and adviser; conversation expresses a *fictional* chain of assurances, not a verified administrative reporting structure.

1. **Company adviser:** “I am assured that the population remains fundamentally loyal.”
2. **Indian ruler:** “By whom?”
3. **Company adviser:** “Our local representatives.”
4. **Indian ruler:** “And who assured them?”
5. **Company adviser:** “Their local representatives.”
6. **Indian ruler:** “Then it seems your government is very popular with its representatives.”

**Future evidence:** Save the original fictional assurances as spoken *claims*; they do not create physical Company agents or prove any genuine reports.

**Guards:**
- CB-R03-01 is a sardonic generalisation about conquest justification, **not** proof that a particular Company official spoke to the ruler.

## 6. Rare emergent eccentricities

Eccentricity is not an arbitrary random tag or guaranteed recurring punchline. It is rare and earned through actual personality, experiences, speech and subsequent behaviour. Dossiers may explain the evidence, without inventing prior acts.

### EC-R03-01 — The proclamation enthusiast

**Conditions:** Ruler has survived repeated relevant Company confrontations and has developed a saved, supported sardonic fascination with Company explanations or proclamations. A collecting *habit* must be established by saved words/actions; merely selecting this line doesn't prove physical proclamations were obtained.

**Ruler, after actual successful rebellion:**
> “Do send me the Company's explanation of this affair. I have grown rather fond of its explanations. They improve remarkably with each defeat.”

This speech can *begin* a new interest. Do not describe a pre-existing collection in the historical dossier without evidence.

### EC-R03-02 — The adviser who cannot admit defeat

**Conditions:** Adviser has a supported pattern of actual failed relevant Company operations, growing self-protective bombast; the latest operation is a real defeat. Do not misattribute command or guilt.

**Adviser, after another actual defeat:**
> “I refuse to describe this as a defeat. It is a temporary redistribution of strategic responsibilities.”

**Later conditional follow-up:** Same adviser has a later confirmed successful operation **and** EC-R03-02 was actually saved as earlier dialogue.

**Adviser, smug after genuine later success:**
> “A decisive victory. You will observe that I have always been very precise in my terminology.”

This is intentional irony, not a narrator-certified pattern of competence.

## Selection and implementation contract

1. Confirm genuine committed event kind, actual outcome, region, sovereignty state, participant eligibility, current branch and previous source references **before** tone/personality weighting.
2. Unrest, failed rebellion and successful rebellion remain **separate facts**. If explicit rebellion resolution isn't recorded, do not activate success/failure scenes.
3. In ordinary play, show chronicler + one or two reactions; occasionally select one complete exchange; extra perspectives expandable. Understated tragedy beats satire after catastrophic consequences.
4. Store exact selected dialogue and its stable ID, expressions, speaker identities, source incident references, audience knowledge and branch-safe links as part of unified transactions. No runtime paraphrasing or remembered speech that never occurred.
5. Treat prior statements as subjective and fallible, but retain their original exact wording for subsequent callbacks; interpretations may evolve.
6. Every ruler gets individual voice, motives and emotional development. Power-seeking and opportunism are allowed; ethnic stereotypes, jokes about civilian tragedy, generic identical patriotic speeches and omniscient advisers are not.
7. Apply contextual cooldowns and rarity of eccentricities; an increasingly ridiculous adviser may still have sincere moments.
8. A line deferred for unavailable facts stays in the authored catalogue for future review but **must not** be made eligible by fabricating a missing game event.
9. Preserve physical-game mechanical equivalence. These lines and plot arcs never choose success/failure or alter troops, conquest, tax, loot or orders.
10. No new application implementation work is authorised by publishing this content; Codex integrates after separate implementation go-ahead.

**Creative ownership:** This collection is approved authored material. Technical integration may change data representation, never silently revise wording or loosen eligibility guards.
