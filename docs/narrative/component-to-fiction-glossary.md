# John Company 2E — component-to-fiction narrative glossary

**Status:** Approved editorial reference, 26 October 2026. Applies to all new narrative writing and future revisions. It does **not** change published game rules, mechanics, saved quotations, or physical instruction text.

## Central rule

The board is an abstraction of the historical world. **Game state selects the fictional situation; characters and the chronicler speak about that world, not the tabletop interface.** A game object can still be called by its proper game name in **factual dialog titles, player instructions, debug UI and rule help**, where clarity is essential. No diegetic narration describes a counter moving or an event card becoming visible.

For dialogue, distinguish (1) authoritative recorded outcomes, (2) reasonable fictional interpretations, and (3) subjective or comic exaggeration by fallible speakers. Minor invented social incidents are welcome. No unreported consequential deaths, actual player payments, campaigns, laws, new control transfers, player-family actions or concluded China trade.

This document includes physical components outside the companion's presently tracked state. Listing a component **does not** mean the app tracks its quantity, owner, availability or individual history. Use only supported actual state for factual claims.

## India and political state

| Physical component or notation | Narrative-world meaning | Specific guard |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Regional tower / tower levels | Relative political authority, military might, courtly power, ability to project force | **Never literal construction, rising towers, new forts or soldiers recruited** unless independently established |
| Dome, capital/sovereignty indicator | A court's status and regional sovereignty, in context | Do not invent physical destruction of a palace dome |
| Large/small empire flags and regional control indicators | Capital, overlordship, subordination, independence, changes in allegiance | A tabletop flag changing is a political transition, not a flag ritual |
| Company control markers / region occupation | Company authority, political domination, administration and territorial control | Do not assume locals welcomed the transfer or that a named governor was installed |
| Region loot token and its £ value | Once-acquired conquest wealth, plunder or captured resources | Only describe received loot on an actual eligible first Company conquest; no invented specific artifact |
| Unrest cubes | Dissatisfaction, grievances, resistance, precarious authority | Cubes are not literal riots; unrest alone does not certify a rebellion |
| Elephant marker and its position/movement | Shifting political attention, looming potential crisis and perceived regional danger | **There is no prophetic elephant marching through India**; no certain future event implied |
| Event tile faces / event stack / visible region | Unfolding public crises and the places under attention | Characters cannot see a stack, reveal a card or know unrevealed fronts |
| Region adjacency/borders | Neighbouring political influence, frontier tension, regional proximity | No automatic alliance, invasion or troop movement from adjacency alone |
| Crisis/Rebellion/Foreign Invasion result | Actual political or military crisis and its recorded consequence | Do not narrate a confirmed war, commander or casualty before actual resolution |
| Peace, Turmoil, Leader, Windfall, Shuffle | Changed stability, commerce, authority, prosperity or uncertainty | Use in-world incidents and attitudes, never component-manipulation terminology |

## Commerce and maritime interests

| Physical component or notation | Narrative-world meaning | Specific guard |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Open / closed / filled Order spaces | Available, disrupted or used commercial opportunities | Do not depict literal doors opening or closing; 'orders' as contracts may be used only naturally |
| Writers | Company trading employees, clerks, agents and commercial representatives | An invented one-off clerk is **not** a claim that a player's specific Writer received money |
| Trade routes / Order links | Networks of markets, trading connections and transport | No implied successful trade or actual journey absent outcome |
| Order £ / trade value | Potential value of a market or transaction | Potential earnings are not realized receipts |
| Opium symbols on map | Regional productive or supply potential available under Company control | Never count physical icons in dialogue; actual China cargo/profit isn't tracked |
| Superintendent of Trade in China office | A Company office authorized for China affairs | Establishing an office is **not** successful trade, arrival of ships or proceeds |
| Ships (Company/private/extra) | Maritime vessels, ownership or temporary shipping availability | Distinguish tracked availability from untracked actual cargo |
| Fatigued ships / storm die | Temporarily limited shipping and weather hazards | Storm die is not an object in-fiction, and the die does not establish an actual named vessel's fate |

## Military actions

| Physical component or notation | Narrative-world meaning | Specific guard |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Presidency Army boxes | Bombay, Madras and Bengal military establishments | Boxes are not buildings, nor evidence of exact troop totals |
| Regiments and officers | Soldiers/organized units and Company officers | Do not invent unit rosters or an actual numbered army strength |
| Exhausted Army pieces | Forces already committed and temporarily unable to contribute | Exhaustion is **not** mass injury, death or annihilation |
| Commander position/removal | Command authority, dismissal or removal from office as recorded | Avoid assuming execution, death or disgrace beyond the actual reported effect |
| Local alliance tokens | Participating independent Indian groups, courts, bankers or allied military interests | Alliance participation only if committed/reported; their political motives can be fictional |
| Die/dice, successes and calculated strength | Mechanical uncertainty, battlefield odds, relative military prospects | No one in historical India rolls game dice to fight. A large final pool can inspire apparent confidence, not a literal troop inventory |
| Trophy tokens | Recognition, reputation and military prestige | Not necessarily a tangible cup or medal |

## Company finances, offices and shareholders

| Physical component or notation | Narrative-world meaning | Specific guard |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Company Standing track | Public/political credibility and confidence | No fictional reputation 'marker'; exact position can inform mood |
| Company Balance/Debt | Treasury resources and borrowing obligations | Neither implies individual shareholder payouts |
| Shares | Ownership stakes and investor exposure | Don't pretend to know specific player family holdings |
| Dividends and emergency loans | Reported distributions and Company borrowing | Only narrate actual reported Revenue facts |
| Offices, Governor, Director, Chairman, President, Governor General | Institutional authority and real officeholders | A persistent fictional adviser is not automatically the player holding the office |
| Office fatigue/exhaustion cubes | Limited remaining institutional capacity for actions | Avoid literal exhausted board pieces |
| Bonuses and office treasuries | Rewards, remuneration and control of certain funds | Don't invent who was paid or a family transfer |
| One-off Company clerks, shareholders, secretaries | Fictional social life, frustration and ambition in the enterprise | No automatic persistent NPC creation or player-specific identity |

## Player families, politics and game procedure

| Physical component or notation | Narrative-world meaning | Specific guard |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Family member pieces and family boards | Members and fortunes of influential British families | The companion does not comprehensively track them |
| Promise/Blackmail/Prestige cards | Influence, favours, compromising information, status | Cards aren't literal documents unless a narrative-specific basis exists; don't invent player deals |
| Enterprises, private firms and investment pieces | Commercial ventures, patronage and wealth | No unreported profits or expenditure |
| Retirement prizes and victory points | Social standing, estate, ambition and victory abstraction | A character never says 'victory points' |
| Laws, voting pawn, Prime Minister/Opposition markers | Legislation, parliamentary negotiation and political leverage | A fictional MP's speech is not an enacted law or completed vote |
| Turn/phase markers, scenario cards, Crown automa aids | Passage of time, gameplay procedure, variant political roles | No fictional in-world marker, deck or AI mechanism |
| Money coins | Currency/financial transfers | Currency exists historically, but payments only when genuinely established |

## Narrative tests for editors and Codex

1. **Could this line be spoken or written by a person in the fictional historical world?** If it refers to the Elephant, a tower level, an unrest cube, an Order space, an event tile, a die pool or the game phase, rewrite the fictional speech.
2. **Does the author distinguish a symbol from a concrete incident?** A tower increase does not prove troops were recruited; unrest does not prove an uprising; a Windfall does not prove a specific player Writer got paid.
3. **Is the claim consequential?** Be exact for territorial changes, offices, deaths, laws, loans, real pay-outs and reported outcomes. Feel free to invent incidental clerks, petty misunderstandings and social humour.
4. **Is the knowledge available at that point?** Private thoughts stay private. Parliament or shareholders cannot discuss future India Events. The Revenue meeting may know *current-turn completed Company operations* and *previous-turn India Events*.
5. **Do not rewrite saved accepted lines behind the user's back.** Flag questionable existing texts, review exact replacements creatively, and only then update templates/migrations if required.
6. **Keep player instruction clarity.** 'Increase Delhi's tower by one level' belongs in the physical rules UI, never in a ruler's speech.

## Illustrative translations (new examples, not approved production prose)

- Tower level rises in Delhi → 'Delhi's influence had become difficult for its neighbours to ignore.'
- Elephant marker moves toward Bengal with high unrest → 'Fresh dispatches from Bengal were accompanied by unusually emphatic assurances that the province remained tranquil.'
- Event stack publicly indicates Madras → 'Attention in the southern presidencies had turned uneasily toward Madras.'
- Turmoil closes a commercial Order → 'Merchants complained that their dealings had been interrupted by the latest political difficulties.'
- Large dice pool ends catastrophically → 'An expedition confidently expected to succeed returned with little worth celebrating.'
- Exhausted Army cannot defend another region → 'The Presidency's available forces had already been committed elsewhere.'
- No Revenue dividend → 'The proprietors greeted the accounts with the disappointment peculiar to men awaiting a payment that had not materialized.'
