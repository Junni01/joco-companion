# Parliamentary humour and political absurdities during the East India Company era

**Research and creative reference · 26 October 2026.** **Design inspiration / writing candidates only**, not authorised production prose, extra Parliamentary mechanics, or changes to the ongoing Character Engine M2B-1 task.

## Historical source and fictional campaign boundary

This catalogue records documented parliamentary situations and their satirical mechanisms. An event in real British history is **not** automatically an occurrence of our alternate-history campaign. Our comic fictional characters can express vanity, contradictions and partisan claims, but the chronicler must not confuse them with authoritative outcomes. Parliament remains physically resolved by players: report only laws/dilemmas relevant to tracked state. Use one complete scrollable narrative scene rather than mandatory micro-dialogue controls. See [master backlog](../PRODUCT-IDEAS-BACKLOG.md), [09C/09D narrative contract](collection09cd-counsel-dialogues.md), [historical EIC catalogue](historical-humour-inspiration-catalogue.md).

### PHI-01 — Fox's India Bill and the fall of the coalition (1783)
- **Kernel:** Fox's bill to replace much of the Company's political administration with parliamentary commissioners passed the Commons but failed in the Lords amid King George III's opposition, and the Fox–North coalition was dismissed.
- **Comic mechanism:** politicians who promise to discipline Company misgovernment become embroiled in a greater struggle over their own authority; rivals cooperate until their alliance collapses.
- **Potential trigger:** law affecting the Company, a Parliament opening conversation after an especially embarrassing India event; fictional MPs may blame one another for failed measures but **must not declare a failed vote** absent such a reported outcome.
- **Sources:** [UK Parliament — India and British politics](https://www.parliament.uk/about/living-heritage/evolutionofparliament/legislativescrutiny/parliament-and-empire/), [British Museum collection search — Carlo Khan](https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/search?keyword=Carlo%20Khan).

### PHI-02 — The “mince pie administration” and Pitt's longevity (1783 onward)
- **Kernel:** Opponents derided the young Pitt ministry as unlikely to endure, but Pitt remained Prime Minister from 1783 to 1801 (and later returned).
- **Comic mechanism:** a pompous confident forecast contradicted by reality years later.
- **Potential trigger:** *only* when an actual prior **saved statement** predicts a Company political disaster or longevity and later committed facts rebut it. For our John Company game, fictional parliamentary careers need not simulate real prime ministers.
- **Source:** [UK Parliament — William Pitt the Younger](https://www.parliament.uk/about/living-heritage/evolutionofparliament/legislativescrutiny/parliament-and-empire/).

### PHI-03 — The seven-year impeachment of Warren Hastings (1788–1795)
- **Kernel:** Hastings's Westminster Hall impeachment trial lasted from 1788 to 1795 and concluded in acquittal; the allegations concerned severe matters of imperial administration and suffering in India.
- **Comic mechanism:** an inquiry with elaborate procedures and endless hearings outlasts the very official's former career.
- **Potential trigger:** only a legitimately recorded recurring fictional scandal/inquiry; do not create prosecution, trial verdict, dismissal or fine out of an unrelated game event.
- **Source:** [UK Parliament — Warren Hastings impeachment history](https://www.parliament.uk/about/living-heritage/evolutionofparliament/legislativescrutiny/parliament-and-empire/).

### PHI-04 — Edmund Burke, Sheridan and parliamentary rhetorical theatre
- **Kernel:** Burke's denunciations in the Hastings case and Sheridan's famously extensive speech concerning the Begums of Oudh exemplify the extraordinary political rhetoric of the period.
- **Comic mechanism:** grandiose speeches, elaborate insults, and a clerk calmly requesting the actual proposition.
- **Potential trigger:** actual India-phase conflict, law/dilemma debate or scandal; a speaker's theatrical accusation is *not* certified by the chronicler.
- **Source:** [UK Parliament — Hastings impeachment](https://www.parliament.uk/about/living-heritage/evolutionofparliament/legislativescrutiny/parliament-and-empire/).

### PHI-05 — MPs with direct financial interests in the East India Company
- **Kernel:** Eighteenth-century MPs and EIC directors/shareholders included overlapping individuals. Confirm exact percentages, dates and sample before putting numerical claims in production commentary.
- **Comic mechanism:** 'entirely disinterested' defence of an institution in which a speaker is personally invested.
- **Potential trigger:** recurring Old Sarum MP in shareholder/Parliament scenes, during supported dividends, loans and state intervention; the fictionally suspected holding is **authored character background**, not an unreported player family investment.
- **Source:** [UK Parliament — East India Company and public opinion](https://www.parliament.uk/about/living-heritage/evolutionofparliament/legislativescrutiny/parliament-and-empire/parliament-and-the-american-colonies-before-1765/the-east-india-company-and-public-opinion/).

### PHI-06 — The Company financial crisis, government loans and oversight (1772–1773)
- **Kernel:** Corporate financial distress and parliamentary intervention occurred despite Company's growing political power in India.
- **Comic mechanism:** managing territorial sovereignty while failing to reconcile business accounts; celebrating government aid as evidence of sound management.
- **Potential trigger:** genuinely recorded Revenue emergency loans, dividends, Standing, or terminal Company failure, but not invented money.
- **Source:** [UK Parliament — Parliament and the East India Company](https://www.parliament.uk/about/living-heritage/evolutionofparliament/legislativescrutiny/parliament-and-empire/parliament-and-the-american-colonies-before-1765/parliament-and-the-east-india-company/).

### PHI-07 — Pitt's Board of Control and overlapping authority (1784)
- **Kernel:** Pitt's India Act added government supervision through a Board of Control while leaving substantial Company machinery in place.
- **Comic mechanism:** every institution insists it is in charge until something fails, when all insist the other is responsible.
- **Potential trigger:** parliamentary disputes after government or Company actions, applicable law (not a new mechanical Board of Control office unless real game rules require it).
- **Source:** [UK Parliament — British administration of India overview](https://www.parliament.uk/about/living-heritage/evolutionofparliament/legislativescrutiny/parliament-and-empire/).

### PHI-08 — Satirical prints of “Carlo Khan” and India Bill politics (1783–1784)
- **Kernel:** James Sayers and other artists mocked Charles James Fox's India Bill, depicting Fox as a supposed Oriental despot in “Carlo Khan's Triumphal Entry into Leadenhall Street.”
- **Comic mechanism:** ambitious political reformers accused of coveting the very imperial powers they claim to restrain.
- **Potential trigger:** elite rival nickname and political accusation, separately recorded as partisan rhetoric; avoid adopting racist visual caricatures from period print culture.
- **Source:** [British Museum — Carlo Khan search](https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/search?keyword=Carlo%20Khan).

### PHI-09 — Rotten/pocket boroughs, Old Sarum and parliamentary patronage
- **Kernel — well supported:** The **real Old Sarum** near Salisbury had very few electors and nevertheless returned **two MPs** until its representation was removed under the **1832 Reform Act**. Pre-reform patronage and absence of a secret ballot enabled wealthy interests to exercise disproportionate influence. **Do not assert a constant exact electorate size**; historical figures differ by year and voter qualifications.
- **Comic mechanism:** an MP grandly claims the unanimous support of 'the nation' while talking about the private wishes of a tiny electorate or local patron.
- **Approved creative direction (26 October 2026):** Give our **fictional recurring, Company-friendly rotten-borough MP** the genuine constituency **Old Sarum**. He has an immutable identity once introduced, is cunning and plausibly informed rather than stupid, can be sincerely accurate on some policy points, and is extraordinarily resourceful in denying his personal conflict of interest. The real borough is historical colour **not a new election/voting minigame**. A name, personal backstory, confirmed fictional EIC investments, electoral relationship, and exact lines are still **PROPOSED / to be reviewed**; don't invent a player shareholder.
- **Blackadder reference:** *Blackadder the Third* (BBC, 1987), **series 3 episode 1 “Dish and Dishonesty,”** dramatizes an MP elected via the **fictional** rotten borough Dunny-on-the-Wold, not Old Sarum. The plot inserts Baldrick to help defeat William Pitt the Younger's plan to cut the Prince Regent's funding. **Borrow the high-level satirical device, not the copyrighted dialogue or named Blackadder characters.**
- **East India Company in Blackadder:** No specific direct EIC mention verified in the checked guides/scripts; the third-series episode “Duel and Duality” includes a joke about fetching tea from India/China, which is not a direct Company reference. **Do not claim Blackadder explicitly satirized the EIC.**
- **Sources:** [UK National Archives — 1832 Reform Act](https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/education/teaching-resources/1832-great-reform-act/); [BBC subtitles — Dish and Dishonesty](https://subsaga.com/bbc/comedy/blackadder/blackadder-the-third/1-dish-and-dishonesty.html); [ITVX episode listing](https://www.itv.com/watch/blackadder/2a7295/2a7295a0013).
- **Era guard:** A game or future standalone simulation set after 1832 must not present Old Sarum as an active constituency with an MP, unless clearly marked intentional alternate-history divergence. In current John Company eras, carefully verify scenario chronology.

### PHI-10 — Admiralty funds, Henry Dundas and Melville's impeachment (1805–1806)
- **Kernel:** The senior politician Henry Dundas (Lord Melville), who had also shaped policy involving India, was impeached over naval financial management. Contemporary proceedings involved disputed handling of accounts and evidence; not every allegation was proven.
- **Comic mechanism:** expensive bureaucracy and evasive declarations that discussing a missing sum would itself be contrary to public interest.
- **Potential trigger:** naval/shipping clerk, scandal/inquiry in fictional London setting. Do not make a historically disputed charge into established guilt.
- **Source:** [Hansard historical debate archive](https://hansard.parliament.uk/).

### PHI-11 — A trading corporation steadily loses its right to trade (1813–1833)
- **Kernel:** Parliament progressively curtailed the EIC's commercial monopolies; the 1833 Charter Act ended its commercial operations while it retained administrative functions.
- **Comic mechanism:** the 'Company' can no longer trade but continues to govern vast territories.
- **Potential trigger:** mainly future standalone/late-era content; John Company companion must not imply abolition occurred prematurely.
- **Source:** [UK Parliament — historical India and charter material](https://www.parliament.uk/about/living-heritage/evolutionofparliament/legislativescrutiny/parliament-and-empire/).

## Sketch: recurring Old Sarum MP (creative proposal, not a character generation instruction)

- **Identity:** fictional persistent MP, place = Old Sarum, introduced only at an appropriate London Parliament scene, not a tracked player officeholder. A fictional sponsor/landowner may be mentioned only if established by a saved backstory, not invented each turn.
- **Voice:** unfailingly formal, witty legalisms, hollow declarations of public duty, increasingly ingenious excuses, occasional principled positions that happen to favour Company interests; **not** a copy of Baldrick, Blackadder or a named real MP.
- **Recurring concerns:** Company dividends, urgent requests for state assistance, anti-Company criticism, successful conquests, French setbacks and possible legislation. His positions evolve via his earlier *saved, actually spoken statements*. He might change policy stance but insist no contradiction occurred.
- **Scene rhythm:** not automatically in every Parliament meeting; sometimes make him the opening speaker, sometimes a heckler, sometimes let another fictional MP lampoon him, and sometimes omit him entirely.
- **Long-game change:** plausible seat loss at 1832 Reform (if modeled in a longer game), continuing as former MP/person rather than disappearance. In the current companion no procedural election result is introduced.
- **Sample entirely ORIGINAL candidate, not approved production text:**
  > MP: “My constituents are unanimous.”  
  > Opposition member: “How many did you consult?”  
  > MP: “The proprietor, whose judgment has always spared them the inconvenience of disagreement.”  
  > Opposition member: “But you are the proprietor.”  
  > MP: “We enjoy a remarkable closeness of opinion.”
- **Source timing:** Parliament occurs after the current India event phase; the MP may discuss those *completed* events. A Revenue shareholder scene occurs earlier and can discuss current completed Company Operations, previous India's events, and current actually reported financial outcome. No knowledge of future or privately held deck order.
